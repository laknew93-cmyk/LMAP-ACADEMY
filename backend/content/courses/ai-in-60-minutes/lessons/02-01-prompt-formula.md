You have seen that detailed prompts get better answers. Now you will learn a simple four-part formula that makes this automatic, so you never have to wonder what to type.

## Why vague prompts disappoint

Compare these two prompts:

```text
Write a post about my shop.
```

```text
You are a social media writer for small Indian businesses. Write an Instagram caption for my home bakery in Pune announcing our new eggless Diwali hampers, priced from ₹799. Our customers are mostly young families. Keep it under 60 words, warm and festive, and end with 3 relevant hashtags.
```

The first gets a generic paragraph that could be about any shop anywhere. The second gets something you could post almost as is. The difference is not magic; it is four ingredients.

## The formula: Role, Task, Context, Format

### 1. Role: who should the AI act as?

Giving the AI a role sets the tone and expertise level.

- "You are an experienced HR manager..."
- "You are a patient maths tutor for a Class 9 student..."
- "You are a friendly customer support executive..."

### 2. Task: what exactly do you want?

Use a clear action word: write, summarise, explain, list, compare, plan, rewrite, suggest.

- "Write a cover letter..."
- "Compare these two phone plans..."
- "List 10 interview questions..."

### 3. Context: what does the AI need to know?

This is the ingredient beginners skip most. Think about who it is for, why, and any constraints.

- Who is the audience?
- What is the background or situation?
- What should be avoided?
- Any budget, deadline or word limit?

### 4. Format: how should the answer look?

Tell it the shape you want: bullet points, a table, a short paragraph, a WhatsApp-length message, a step-by-step list, or a fixed word count.

## A template you can reuse

```text
You are a [ROLE].
[TASK: what you want, starting with an action word].
Context: [who it's for, background, constraints].
Format: [bullets / table / paragraph, length, tone].
```

You do not need to use these exact labels. Once you are comfortable, you will write it as natural sentences. But the labels are a helpful checklist in the beginning.

## The formula in action

**Rohit, a job seeker in Indore:**

```text
You are a career coach who understands the Indian job market.
Rewrite my LinkedIn "About" section to attract recruiters for operations manager roles.
Context: I have 6 years of experience in warehouse operations at a logistics company, I led a team of 25, and I reduced dispatch delays significantly. I am open to roles in Pune or Bengaluru.
Format: First person, under 200 words, confident but not boastful.
```

**Meena, returning to work after a career break:**

```text
You are a supportive career counsellor.
Help me explain a 4-year career break in a job interview.
Context: I took the break to care for my young children. Before that I was an accounts executive. During the break I completed an online Tally and GST course.
Format: Give me a short, honest answer I can say aloud in about 30 seconds, plus 2 tips for delivering it.
```

**Arjun, a Class 12 student:**

```text
You are a patient chemistry teacher.
Explain the difference between ionic and covalent bonds.
Context: I am preparing for my board exams and find this topic confusing.
Format: A simple comparison table, then 3 practice questions with answers at the end.
```

## Two bonus tips

**Give an example.** If you want a particular style, paste a sample: "Here is a message I liked. Write in a similar style." AI is very good at matching examples.

**Ask it to ask you.** If you are not sure what context to give, add: "Before you answer, ask me any questions you need to do this well." The AI will interview you, and the final answer will be much better.

## Try it yourself

About 15 minutes.

1. Pick one weak prompt you used in Lesson 3, or write a vague one like "help me with my resume."
2. Rewrite it using all four parts: role, task, context and format.
3. Send the original vague prompt in one new chat, and your improved prompt in another.
4. Compare the two answers side by side. Which would you actually use?
5. Try once more with the bonus tip: add "Before you answer, ask me any questions you need." Answer its questions and see the result.
6. Save your best prompt in your phone's notes app as a reusable template.

## Key takeaways

- Strong prompts have four parts: role, task, context and format.
- Context is the most commonly skipped part and often makes the biggest difference.
- Telling the AI the format you want saves editing time later.
- Pasting an example helps the AI match the style you like.
- When unsure, ask the AI to ask you questions first.
