AI assistants are useful, but they are not a locked diary and they are not always right. This lesson covers the two habits that keep you safe: protecting what you share, and checking what you get back.

## Part 1: What never to paste into AI

Anything you type into an AI chat is sent to the company's servers. Depending on your settings, it may be stored, reviewed for safety, or used to improve their models. A simple rule: **if you would not post it on a public notice board, do not paste it into a free AI chat.**

### Never share

| Type of information | Examples |
|---|---|
| Passwords and codes | Email, banking or UPI passwords, UPI PIN, OTPs |
| Financial details | Card numbers, CVV, bank account numbers, net banking IDs |
| Government IDs | Aadhaar number, PAN, passport number, voter ID |
| Health records | Lab reports or prescriptions with your name on them |
| Other people's private data | A customer list with phone numbers, an employee's salary slip |
| Confidential work material | Client contracts, unreleased plans, internal financials |

### If you need help with a document, anonymise it first

You can still get help with sensitive tasks. Just remove or replace identifying details before pasting:

- Replace names with "Client A" or "Employee 1".
- Remove phone numbers, addresses, account numbers and ID numbers.
- Round or change exact figures if the precise number does not matter.

For example, a Chennai CA firm's article assistant who wants help rewording a notice reply can replace the client's name, PAN and GSTIN with placeholders, get the improved wording, and put the real details back in afterwards.

### At work, check your company's policy

Many companies have rules about which AI tools staff may use and what data can go into them. Some provide an approved business version. If you are unsure, ask before pasting anything work-related.

## Part 2: Hallucinations and how to catch them

In Lesson 1 you learned that AI can produce confident answers that are simply wrong. These are called hallucinations. They happen because the AI predicts likely-sounding text; it does not check facts the way a person would.

### Where hallucinations are most common

- **Specific numbers**: statistics, dates, prices, tax rates, interest rates.
- **Names and sources**: book titles, research papers, court judgments, website links.
- **Recent events**: news, new rules, or changes after the model's training.
- **Niche local details**: a small shop's timings, a specific college's cut-off.

### Warning signs

- Very precise figures with no source.
- A reference or link you cannot find when you search for it.
- An answer that changes when you ask the same question again.
- Something that sounds too neat or too good to be true.

## Part 3: A simple fact-check routine

Use this whenever an answer matters, especially for money, health, law, exams or anything you will share publicly.

1. **Ask for sources.** "What are your sources for this? Which official website should I check?" Then visit them yourself.
2. **Verify on official sites.** For tax rules, check the official income tax or GST portals. For exam dates, the board or university website. For health, a qualified doctor.
3. **Cross-check with a second assistant.** Ask Gemini what ChatGPT told you, or vice versa. Agreement is not proof, but disagreement is a red flag.
4. **Ask the AI to doubt itself.** "Which parts of your answer are you least sure about?" It will often point out weaker claims.
5. **Use a human expert for big decisions.** AI can help you prepare better questions for your CA, doctor or lawyer, but it should not replace them.

```text
Review your previous answer. List any facts, numbers or claims I should verify before relying on them, and suggest where I could check each one.
```

## Try it yourself

About 10-15 minutes.

1. Open your AI assistant and check your privacy settings again. Have you decided on chat history and model-improvement options?
2. Take a real document or message you might want help with. Make an anonymised copy by replacing names, numbers and IDs with placeholders.
3. Ask the AI a factual question in an area you know well, such as your city, your job or your subject.
4. Look for any errors in the answer. Then paste the prompt above and see what the AI flags.
5. Verify one fact from the answer on an official website.

## Key takeaways

- Never paste passwords, OTPs, financial details, government IDs or other people's private data.
- Anonymise documents by replacing names and numbers with placeholders before asking for help.
- Hallucinations are most common with numbers, sources, recent events and local details.
- For anything important, verify on official sites and consult a qualified person.
- Asking the AI which parts it is unsure about is a quick, useful check.
