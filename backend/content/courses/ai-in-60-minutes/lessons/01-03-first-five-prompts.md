This is where AI stops being theory and starts saving you time. You will learn five prompts that cover most everyday needs, and use each one on a real task from your own life.

## 1. Write an email or message

Most of us lose time staring at a blank screen. AI is great at the first draft.

```text
Write a polite email to my landlord, Mr. Sharma, asking him to fix a leaking bathroom tap. It has been leaking for a week. Keep it short and respectful, and ask if a plumber can come this Saturday.
```

Notice what makes this work: who it is for, what the problem is, the tone, and the ask. If the reply is too formal, just say "make it a bit friendlier." You can keep refining in the same chat.

## 2. Summarise something long

Paste in a long text and ask for the key points. This works for school circulars, society notices, policy documents and long email threads (remove private details first; more on that in Module 2).

```text
Summarise the text below in 5 bullet points. Then tell me if there is anything I need to do, and by when.

[paste text here]
```

That second line is the secret. Asking "what do I need to do?" turns a summary into an action list. Many assistants also let you upload a PDF instead of pasting text.

## 3. Explain something confusing

AI is a patient teacher. You can ask the same question five times without embarrassment.

```text
Explain what a mutual fund SIP is, as if I am a complete beginner. Use a simple everyday example with rupees. Keep it under 150 words.
```

If it still feels complicated, follow up with "explain that more simply" or "give me another example." Students can use this for tough concepts in physics or accounts; working professionals can use it for unfamiliar jargon in a meeting.

Remember: for money, health or legal matters, use AI to understand the basics, then confirm with a qualified person or official source.

## 4. Plan something

AI is excellent at turning a fuzzy goal into a structured plan.

```text
Help me plan a 3-day family trip from Bengaluru to Mysuru and Coorg for 2 adults and 2 kids aged 6 and 10. We prefer relaxed mornings and want one nature activity per day. Give a day-by-day plan in a table.
```

The same pattern works for a study timetable before exams, a weekly meal plan, or a launch plan for a small business's new product. Always check timings, prices and opening hours yourself, since the AI may not have current information.

## 5. Rewrite or improve

You write something rough; AI polishes it.

```text
Rewrite this message to a customer so it sounds professional but warm. Keep it short enough for WhatsApp.

"sorry order late, courier issue, will reach by thursday pakka"
```

You can also ask it to:

- Fix grammar without changing the meaning
- Make something shorter
- Translate into Hindi, Tamil, Marathi or another language
- Change the tone (more formal, more friendly, more confident)

## Quick reference

| Need | Start your prompt with |
|---|---|
| Email or message | "Write a [tone] email/message to [person] about..." |
| Summary | "Summarise the text below in [number] bullet points..." |
| Explanation | "Explain [topic] as if I am a beginner, with an example..." |
| Plan | "Help me plan [goal] for [who], with [constraints]..." |
| Rewrite | "Rewrite this to sound [tone], keeping it [length]..." |

## The follow-up is where the magic happens

Beginners often accept the first answer. Instead, treat it like a conversation:

- "Make it shorter."
- "Add a line thanking them."
- "Give me two more options."
- "That is too formal, I am writing to a friend."

Two or three follow-ups usually turn a decent answer into a great one.

## Try it yourself

About 15 minutes. Use the list of three tasks you wrote in Lesson 1.

1. Open your AI assistant and start a new chat.
2. Pick the prompt type that fits your first task (email, summary, explain, plan or rewrite).
3. Adapt the example prompt above with your real details. Avoid personal information like phone numbers or account details.
4. Send it, then make at least **two follow-up requests** to improve the answer.
5. Repeat for your other two tasks in new chats.
6. Note which result saved you the most time.

## Key takeaways

- Five prompt types (email, summarise, explain, plan, rewrite) cover most everyday needs.
- Specific details such as who, what, tone and length produce better answers.
- Asking "what do I need to do?" turns a summary into an action list.
- Follow-up requests improve answers quickly; do not stop at the first reply.
- Check facts, prices and timings yourself, especially for money, health and travel.
