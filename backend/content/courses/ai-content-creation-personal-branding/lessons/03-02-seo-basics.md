Search engine optimisation (SEO) helps the right people find your articles when they search on Google or other search engines. You do not need to become a technical expert. In this lesson you will learn the SEO basics that matter for creators and small brands, and how to use AI for keyword research and on-page optimisation without falling into the trap of mass-produced content.

## How search works, in plain terms

When someone types "how to register a home bakery in Maharashtra", a search engine tries to show the pages that best answer that question. It looks at whether your page is relevant to the search, whether it is genuinely helpful and trustworthy, and whether it is easy to read and loads well.

Google has said publicly that it aims to reward helpful, people-first content regardless of whether AI was used to produce it, and that content produced mainly to manipulate rankings is against its spam policies. In practice, that means mass-producing thin AI articles is a poor strategy. One genuinely useful article beats twenty generic ones.

## Step 1: Find what your audience searches for

Keywords are simply the words people type into search. Good keywords for small creators are usually:

- **Specific**: "GST registration for freelancers" rather than "GST".
- **Question-shaped**: "is ghee good for weight loss", "how much does a wedding photographer cost in Jaipur".
- **Local** where relevant: "Pune", "near me", a neighbourhood or state.
- **Matched to what you offer**.

Ways to find them without paid tools:

- Type your topic into Google and note the autocomplete suggestions.
- Look at the "People also ask" questions and related searches.
- Note the questions clients, students and followers ask you.
- Use free tools such as Google Search Console (once your site is live) to see which searches already bring visitors.

AI can help you brainstorm, but it does not have reliable data on actual search volumes. Treat its suggestions as ideas to check, not facts.

```text
I write for [audience] about [topic]. List 25 specific questions this
audience might type into Google, grouped by intent:
- learning (how/what/why)
- comparing (X vs Y, best X for Y)
- ready to act (cost, near me, hire, buy)
Use the plain words a non-expert in India would use, including common
Hinglish phrasing where natural. Note that I will verify these with
Google autocomplete.
```

## Step 2: Match the search intent

Before writing, search your chosen keyword yourself and look at the top results. Are they guides, lists, product pages, videos? This tells you what searchers want. If every top result for "best laptop for students under 50000" is a comparison list, a personal essay will struggle to rank.

## Step 3: Optimise the page

These on-page basics cover most of what a beginner needs:

| Element | What to do |
|---|---|
| Title | Include the main keyword naturally; keep it clear and under about 60 characters |
| Meta description | A 1–2 sentence summary that encourages clicks; include the keyword |
| URL | Short and readable, e.g. /gst-registration-freelancers |
| Headings | Use H2/H3 subheadings that reflect the questions people ask |
| Opening | Answer the main question early; do not bury it |
| Internal links | Link to your other related articles |
| External links | Link to official or reliable sources for facts |
| Images | Compress them and add descriptive alt text |
| Author info | Show who wrote it and why they are qualified |

Ask AI to help with the mechanical parts:

```text
Here is my article draft. The main keyword is "[keyword]".
1. Suggest 5 title options under 60 characters that include it naturally.
2. Write 3 meta descriptions of 140–155 characters.
3. Suggest a short URL slug.
4. Check whether my H2 headings cover the main questions searchers
   might have; suggest any missing ones.
5. Suggest alt text for these images: [describe images].
Do not change the body text.
```

## Step 4: Show experience and trust

Search engines and readers both value content written by someone with real experience. Make yours visible:

- Mention first-hand experience: "In the eight years I have been doing GST filings for small businesses in Chennai..."
- Include original examples, photos and screenshots (with private data removed).
- Keep an author bio with your real credentials.
- Update articles when rules change, and show the updated date.

This is especially important for topics like health, money and law, where wrong information can hurt people.

## Step 5: Be patient and keep improving

SEO is slow. A new article on a new website may take weeks or months to appear in search results. Once you have Google Search Console set up, check which searches show your pages and which get clicks. If an article appears for a question you did not answer fully, add a section answering it.

## What to avoid

- **Keyword stuffing**: repeating the keyword awkwardly. Write for humans.
- **Mass-produced AI articles**: many thin pages on similar topics.
- **Copying competitors' articles**: even reworded with AI, this adds no value and can raise copyright issues.
- **Buying backlinks or using link schemes**: these violate search engine guidelines.
- **Chasing every trending keyword** that has nothing to do with what you offer.

## Try it yourself

Spend 15–20 minutes applying SEO basics to the article you started in Lesson 3.1.

1. Run the keyword brainstorming prompt for your topic.
2. Check your three favourite questions in Google autocomplete and "People also ask"; choose one main keyword.
3. Search that keyword and note the format of the top five results.
4. Run the on-page optimisation prompt on your draft.
5. Edit your title, headings and opening so the main question is answered early.
6. Add one line that shows your first-hand experience and one link to an official source.

## Key takeaways

- SEO helps the right readers find genuinely helpful content; thin, mass-produced AI content is a poor bet.
- Find specific, question-shaped keywords using autocomplete, "People also ask" and real client questions.
- Check search intent by looking at what already ranks before you write.
- Cover on-page basics: title, meta description, URL, headings, links and alt text.
- Show real experience and keep articles updated, especially on money, health and legal topics.
