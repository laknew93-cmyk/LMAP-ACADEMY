A content calendar turns good intentions into a plan you can actually follow. In this lesson you will use AI to build Project 2: a realistic 30-day content calendar based on your pillars, your available time and the Indian festival and work calendar.

## Start with capacity, not ambition

The most common mistake is planning too much. Someone plans a post every day across four platforms, manages it for ten days, then disappears for three months. Consistency beats volume. A modest plan you keep for a year builds far more trust than an ambitious plan you abandon.

Before opening any AI tool, answer honestly:

- How many hours a week can I spend on content, including editing and replying to comments?
- Which one or two platforms matter most for my audience?
- Which formats am I comfortable with right now (text, carousel, short video, long-form)?

A sensible starting point for most working professionals is three LinkedIn posts a week, or three Instagram posts plus one reel. A small brand with a part-time social media manager might manage four to five posts a week across two platforms. Choose a number that feels slightly easy.

## Design a weekly rhythm

A rhythm assigns each pillar or format to a regular day. It removes daily decisions and makes your content feel organised to followers.

Here is an example for Riya, a UX designer in Pune building her LinkedIn presence:

| Day | Pillar | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday | Expertise: design tips for product teams | Carousel |
| Thursday | Experience: lessons from projects | Story post |
| Saturday | Perspective: opinions on design careers | Short text post |

And for a Pune bakery's Instagram:

| Day | Pillar | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Behind the scenes | Reel of morning baking |
| Wednesday | Product | Photo post with story behind the item |
| Friday | Customer love | Repost of a customer story (with permission) |
| Sunday | Weekend specials | Story series plus one post |

## Add dates that matter to your audience

Indian audiences respond to the calendar around them. Consider:

- **Festivals and occasions**: Diwali, Eid, Onam, Pongal, Durga Puja, Raksha Bandhan, regional new years. Only use the ones that genuinely connect to your audience and brand.
- **Financial and work dates**: quarterly results season, GST and income tax filing deadlines, appraisal cycles, campus placement season, the start of the academic year.
- **Industry events** and awareness days relevant to your niche.
- **Your own milestones**: a launch, a workshop, a work anniversary.

Ask AI for ideas, but verify every date yourself. Festival dates shift each year, and tax deadlines can be extended. AI tools may give outdated or wrong dates.

## Use AI to draft the calendar

Paste your voice guide, audience, pillars and idea bank into a chat, then use a prompt like this:

```text
Create a 30-day content calendar starting [date] for [platforms].
Constraints:
- I can publish [number] posts per week on [days].
- Weekly rhythm: [paste your rhythm table].
- Use ideas from my idea bank where possible; add new ones only if needed.
- Include relevant dates for my audience in India this month: [list any you know].
- Mix formats: [list formats].
For each post give: Date | Day | Platform | Pillar | Format | Working title |
Hook (first line) | Key point | Call to action | Raw material I need to supply
(e.g. a story, photo, data from my work).
Output as a table.
```

The final column, "raw material I need to supply", is the secret. It reminds you that every post needs something only you can provide, such as a client story, a photo from your workshop or a real number.

## Review the draft like an editor

AI calendars often look tidy but contain problems. Check for:

- **Repetition**: several posts making the same point in different words.
- **Weak calls to action**: "Comment below!" on every post. Vary them: ask a specific question, invite people to save the post, point to your newsletter, or have no call to action at all.
- **Impossible workloads**: three videos in one week when you planned one.
- **Missing proof**: a month with no stories or results feels like lecturing.
- **Unverified facts or dates**.

Ask AI to fix specific issues: "Posts 4 and 11 make the same point. Replace post 11 with an idea from the 'mistakes' angle."

## Move it into a working tool

A calendar in a chat window is easy to lose. Copy the table into a spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel), Notion or a simple Trello board. Add columns for **Status** (idea, drafted, designed, scheduled, published) and **Link** once live.

Then set up batching. Rather than creating every day, set aside one or two sessions a week. For example, Sunday evening for drafting the week's posts with AI, and Wednesday lunch for designing visuals. Most platforms, and tools like Meta Business Suite for Instagram and Facebook, let you schedule posts in advance.

Leave room for the unexpected. Keep one slot a week flexible for news in your field or a timely idea.

## Try it yourself

Build Project 2 in about 20 minutes.

1. Decide your weekly capacity, platforms and posting days.
2. Draw up a weekly rhythm table linking days to pillars and formats.
3. List three to five dates that matter to your audience in the next 30 days and verify each one.
4. Run the calendar prompt with your voice guide, pillars and idea bank.
5. Review the draft using the editor checklist and ask AI to fix at least three issues.
6. Copy the final calendar into a spreadsheet or Notion with Status and Link columns, and block two batching sessions in your phone calendar.

## Key takeaways

- Plan for the time you really have; consistency matters more than volume.
- A weekly rhythm ties pillars and formats to fixed days and removes daily decisions.
- Use Indian festivals, work and tax dates only where relevant, and always verify them.
- Add a "raw material I need" column so every post contains something only you can supply.
- Review AI calendars for repetition, weak calls to action and unrealistic workloads, then batch your work.
