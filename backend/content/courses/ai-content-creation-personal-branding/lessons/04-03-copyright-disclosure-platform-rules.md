The fastest way to damage a brand you have built for years is a copyright complaint, a misleading sponsored post or a viral deepfake controversy. This lesson covers the rules creators in India need to understand: copyright, AI disclosure, influencer advertising guidelines and platform policies. It is practical guidance, not legal advice; when money or serious risk is involved, consult a qualified lawyer.

## Copyright basics for creators

Copyright protects original creative works: writing, photos, music, videos, artwork and software. In India this is governed by the Copyright Act, 1957. A few practical principles:

- **Other people's content is not free to use just because it is online.** Images from Google, songs, film clips and other creators' videos are usually protected.
- **Credit is not permission.** Writing "credit to the owner" does not make using someone's photo or video legal.
- **Music is the most common trap.** Use music from your editor's licensed library or the platform's own audio library, and remember that licences can be limited to that platform.
- **Fonts, templates and stock images have licences** too. Check whether commercial use is allowed, especially for client work.

### AI and copyright

The legal position on AI-generated content is still evolving in India and globally. Key points to keep in mind:

- **Ownership of purely AI-generated output is uncertain.** Courts and copyright offices in different countries have taken different views on whether such works can be protected and who owns them. Your own human contribution, such as writing, editing, selection and arrangement, strengthens your claim.
- **Tool terms of service matter.** Read what each AI tool says about who owns the output and how it can be used commercially.
- **Do not prompt for copies.** Asking a generator to reproduce a specific artist's work, a film character, a brand logo or another creator's distinctive content invites infringement problems.
- **Do not paste other people's articles into AI and publish the "rewrite".** It adds nothing and can still infringe.

## Disclosure of AI-generated content

Platforms and governments are moving towards labelling synthetic content, especially realistic images, audio and video. As of now:

- **YouTube** asks creators to disclose when realistic content has been meaningfully altered or synthetically generated, and may add a label.
- **Meta platforms** (Instagram, Facebook) have introduced "AI info" style labels and ask users to label realistic AI-generated video or audio.
- **Other platforms** have their own rules and labels.
- **The Indian government** has issued advisories and has been working on rules to require labelling of synthetically generated information under the IT Rules.

These policies change, so check the current help pages of each platform you use and follow any news on India's IT Rules. A sensible personal policy, regardless of the minimum rules:

| Situation | Disclose? |
|---|---|
| AI helped you draft or edit a post you reviewed | Generally not required; your choice |
| AI illustration clearly stylised (cartoon, abstract) | Not usually required; optional label |
| Realistic AI image of a person, place or event | Yes |
| Cloned voice or AI avatar presenting content | Yes |
| AI dubbing of your own video into another language | Yes, a short note is good practice |
| Altered real footage that could mislead | Yes, and think twice before publishing |

## Influencer and sponsored content rules

If you earn from brand deals, disclosure is mandatory, not optional. In India, the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) has guidelines for influencer advertising, and the Department of Consumer Affairs has issued endorsement guidelines for social media influencers. In essence:

- **Disclose any material connection** with a brand: payment, free products, discounts, family or employment relationships.
- **Make disclosure prominent and clear**, using labels like "Ad", "Sponsored", "Collaboration" or "Partnership" at the start, not hidden among hashtags at the end.
- **For videos**, the disclosure should appear in the video itself, not only in the caption.
- **Do not endorse products you have not used** or claims you cannot support.
- **Be especially careful in sensitive categories** such as finance, health and education; ASCI has additional expectations for creators giving advice in areas like financial products and health.
- **ASCI also expects disclosure when a virtual or AI-generated influencer** is used.

Read the current guidelines directly on the ASCI website before taking on paid work.

## Platform rules that affect AI content

Most platforms prohibit or restrict:

- **Impersonation** of real people or brands
- **Misleading synthetic media**, especially around elections, health and finance
- **Fake engagement**: bought followers, bots, comment pods and automated AI comments
- **Spam**: mass-produced, repetitive or low-value content
- **Misleading thumbnails and titles**

Automating engagement with AI, such as bulk AI comments on others' posts, can lead to restricted reach or suspended accounts.

## Protecting your own content

- Watermark or brand your carousels and graphics subtly.
- Keep your original files and drafts as proof of creation.
- Use platform reporting tools if someone copies your work.
- Be aware that public content may be scraped for AI training; some platforms provide settings related to this, so review your privacy settings.

## Try it yourself

Spend 15 minutes building your compliance checklist.

1. Review the posts and visuals in your 30-day calendar and flag any that use third-party music, images or AI-generated realistic media.
2. Check the current AI disclosure help page on one platform you use and note what it requires.
3. Write your personal AI disclosure policy in three to five lines, using the table above as a guide.
4. Read ASCI's current influencer guidelines summary and note the disclosure labels you will use.
5. Add a "pre-publish checklist" to your brand voice guide: rights checked, facts checked, AI disclosure decided, sponsorship disclosed.

## Key takeaways

- Online content is not free to use; credit is not permission, and music licences are a frequent trap.
- The law on AI-generated content ownership is unsettled; your human contribution and tool terms matter.
- Label realistic synthetic images, voices and videos, and follow each platform's current rules.
- Disclose every brand relationship clearly and prominently, as ASCI and consumer affairs guidelines require.
- Avoid impersonation, fake engagement and spammy mass-produced content.
