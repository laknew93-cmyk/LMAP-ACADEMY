LinkedIn is where Indian professionals get noticed by recruiters, clients and collaborators. Its posts reward clear thinking and real experience. In this lesson you will learn a repeatable structure for LinkedIn posts and a workflow to draft them with AI without sounding like everyone else.

## How LinkedIn posts are read

Most people see your post in a feed, on a phone, between other things. Only the first two or three lines show before the "see more" cut-off. That means:

- The **opening lines** decide whether anyone reads the rest.
- **Short paragraphs** and white space make it readable on mobile.
- The post should deliver **one clear idea**, not five.
- **Comments and saves** from relevant people matter more than likes from strangers.

LinkedIn changes how its feed works from time to time, so avoid chasing tricks such as "never post links" or "post at exactly 8:47 am". Focus on what consistently works: useful, specific, readable posts for a defined audience.

## A simple post structure

Most strong LinkedIn posts follow a pattern like this:

1. **Hook**: one or two lines that create curiosity or state a strong point.
2. **Context**: the situation, problem or story.
3. **Insight**: what you learned or what you recommend.
4. **Specifics**: steps, examples, numbers or a short list.
5. **Close**: a takeaway line, a question, or an invitation.

Here is a short example from Arjun, an HR manager in Gurugram:

```text
I rejected a candidate with a perfect resume last week.

Not because of skills. Because every answer sounded memorised.

When I asked, "Tell me about a project that went wrong," she said
"I'm a perfectionist" and moved on.

Here's what I wish more freshers knew:
→ Interviewers trust you more when you admit a real mistake.
→ The story matters less than what you changed afterwards.
→ "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out" is a good answer.

Your resume gets you the interview. Your honesty gets you the offer.
```

Notice what makes it work: a real situation, a clear opinion, practical points and no filler.

## Five post types to rotate

| Post type | What it does | Example hook |
|---|---|---|
| Story | Builds trust and memorability | "My first client in Surat paid me in sarees. Here's what that taught me." |
| How-to | Shows expertise | "How I prepare for a client pitch in 30 minutes:" |
| Opinion | Sets you apart | "Most 'personal branding' advice is bad for introverts." |
| Lessons list | Easy to save and share | "7 things I learnt after 10 years in pharma sales:" |
| Celebration with substance | Shares wins without bragging | "We just finished our 100th GST audit. The most common error surprised me." |

## The AI drafting workflow

Do not ask AI to "write a viral LinkedIn post about leadership". Instead, give it your raw material and constraints.

**Step 1: Brain dump.** Write or voice-type everything you know about the idea: the story, what happened, what you think. It can be messy. Two hundred words of rough notes are enough.

**Step 2: Structured draft.** Paste your voice guide, then:

```text
Using my voice guide, turn the notes below into a LinkedIn post.
- Length: 120–200 words.
- Structure: hook, context, insight, specifics, close.
- Keep my real details and phrasing wherever possible.
- Short paragraphs, one to two lines each, for mobile reading.
- No hashtags in the body. Maximum one emoji, or none.
- Do not add any facts, numbers or claims that are not in my notes.
NOTES:
[paste brain dump]
```

The instruction "do not add any facts not in my notes" stops the AI from inventing statistics to sound convincing.

**Step 3: Hook options.** Ask for ten alternative opening lines in different styles: a surprising statement, a specific number from the notes, a short story opener, a contrarian opinion, a question. Pick one, or combine two.

**Step 4: Human edit.** Read the draft aloud. Cut anything you would not say in conversation. Add one detail only you would know: the name of the town, what the client said, how you felt. Delete the final line if it is a generic question.

## Hashtags, tags and timing

Use a few relevant hashtags at the end if you like; they are not a growth strategy. Tag people only when they are genuinely part of the story and would be happy to be tagged. Post when your audience is likely to be on LinkedIn; for many Indian professional audiences that is working-day mornings or lunch breaks, but test this against your own analytics rather than relying on general advice.

## Engagement is part of the post

Spend 15 minutes after posting replying thoughtfully to comments. Also leave useful comments on posts by others in your niche. A considered comment on a respected person's post can introduce you to more of the right people than your own post. You can ask AI to help you think of a useful angle for a comment, but write the comment yourself; templated AI comments are easy to spot.

## Try it yourself

In 15–20 minutes, draft the first of the three posts for Project 3.

1. Choose a story or experience idea from your content calendar.
2. Write a 150–250-word brain dump in your own words.
3. Run the drafting prompt with your voice guide.
4. Generate ten hook options and choose or combine the best.
5. Edit by reading aloud. Add one specific detail and remove anything generic.
6. Save the final draft in your calendar spreadsheet with status "drafted". You will write two more posts in the next lesson's exercise.

## Key takeaways

- The first two lines decide whether your LinkedIn post is read; write them last and with care.
- Use the hook, context, insight, specifics, close structure for clarity.
- Rotate story, how-to, opinion, lessons and celebration posts.
- Give AI your raw notes and forbid it from adding facts you did not supply.
- Replying to comments and commenting on others' posts is part of building your presence.
