Most creators burn out because they treat every post as a new project. Repurposing flips this: you invest in one strong idea and turn it into many pieces across platforms. AI makes the adaptation fast. In this lesson you will build a repurposing system that turns one idea into ten pieces of content.

## Why repurposing works

- **Most of your audience did not see it the first time.** Only a fraction of your followers sees any single post.
- **Different people prefer different formats.** Some read newsletters, some watch reels, some save carousels.
- **Repetition builds association.** Saying the same core idea in different ways helps people remember what you stand for.
- **It saves time.** The hardest part of content is thinking. Repurposing reuses the thinking.

Repurposing is not copy-pasting the same text everywhere. Each platform has its own norms, and each piece should feel native to where it appears.

## Start with a "pillar piece"

The best source for repurposing is a substantial piece of content rich with ideas, such as:

- A long-form article or newsletter (Lesson 3.1)
- A recorded talk, webinar or workshop
- A podcast episode or long YouTube video
- A detailed client case study
- Even a 15-minute voice note where you explain your thinking

If you are recording, a transcript is your raw material. Most transcription tools and many video platforms generate transcripts automatically. Correct the errors, especially names and Indian terms.

## The 1-to-10 map

Here is an example. Shalini, a Lucknow-based career coach, writes a newsletter titled "Why your Naukri profile gets views but no calls." From it she creates:

| # | Format | Platform | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Newsletter (original) | Email | The full argument |
| 2 | Story post | LinkedIn | The client whose profile got 200 views but no calls |
| 3 | Carousel | LinkedIn | 6 profile fixes, one per slide |
| 4 | Opinion post | LinkedIn | "Keyword stuffing your profile is backfiring" |
| 5 | Reel / Short | Instagram, YouTube | The single biggest mistake, in 30 seconds |
| 6 | Reel / Short | Instagram, YouTube | Before/after headline rewrite |
| 7 | Quote graphic | Instagram | One memorable line from the newsletter |
| 8 | Poll | LinkedIn | "Which part of your profile do you update least?" |
| 9 | Checklist PDF | Lead magnet | Profile audit checklist for newsletter sign-ups |
| 10 | Blog article | Website | SEO version targeting "naukri profile not getting calls" |

Ten pieces, one core idea, published over two to three weeks.

## Using AI to repurpose

### Step 1: Extract the atoms

First, ask AI to break your pillar piece into its individual ideas:

```text
Below is my [article / transcript]. Extract every distinct idea, story,
tip, opinion, statistic and memorable line as a numbered list. For each,
note whether it is a story, tip, opinion, data point or quote.
Use only what is in the text.
[paste content]
```

These "atoms" are the building blocks for everything else.

### Step 2: Map atoms to formats

```text
Using these atoms and my content calendar's platforms ([list]),
propose 10 repurposed pieces. For each give: format, platform, which
atoms it uses, a working hook, and why this format suits that idea.
Avoid making two pieces that say the same thing in the same way.
```

### Step 3: Adapt with platform-specific prompts

Write each piece in a fresh prompt that includes your voice guide and the platform's norms:

```text
Using my voice guide, turn atoms 3, 5 and 7 into a 30-second Instagram
Reel script. Spoken, casual, with on-screen text for each line.
Hook in the first 3 seconds. Use only the information in these atoms.
```

Reuse the prompts from earlier lessons: the LinkedIn drafting prompt, the carousel prompt and the short-video script prompt. Repurposing is where your library of prompts pays off.

## Platform adaptation checklist

| Platform | Adapt by |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Professional framing, short paragraphs, a clear takeaway |
| Instagram | Visual first, short caption, conversational, relevant hashtags |
| YouTube Shorts | Strong hook, fast pace, searchable title |
| Email | Personal tone, one idea, a reason to reply |
| Blog | Searchable title, headings, complete answer, links |
| WhatsApp Channel or community | Very short, useful, not spammy |

## Spacing and freshness

Do not publish all ten pieces in one day. Spread them over two to four weeks, mixed with other content. Revisit your best-performing pieces every few months and repurpose them again with a new angle or updated examples. Your newer followers will not have seen them.

Keep a simple "repurposing tracker" column in your calendar so you know which pillar piece each post came from. Later, this helps you see which core ideas perform best across platforms.

## Try it yourself

In about 20 minutes, turn one idea into ten.

1. Choose a pillar piece: your article draft from Lesson 3.1, a past talk, or a 10-minute voice note you record now.
2. Run the atom extraction prompt.
3. Run the mapping prompt to get ten repurposed pieces.
4. Draft two of them in full using platform-specific prompts and your voice guide.
5. Add all ten to your 30-day content calendar, spread across at least two weeks, with a note linking them to the pillar piece.

## Key takeaways

- Repurposing reuses your thinking, the hardest part of content creation.
- Start from a rich pillar piece such as an article, talk or transcript.
- Extract idea atoms first, then map them to formats and platforms.
- Adapt each piece to its platform's norms rather than copy-pasting.
- Spread repurposed pieces over weeks and revisit top performers later.
