Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts are some of the fastest ways to reach new people, but short video is unforgiving: viewers decide within seconds whether to keep watching. In this lesson you will learn how short scripts are structured, use AI to write them, and create Project 4: a short-video script with several hook variants to test.

## Why the hook carries so much weight

On short-video platforms, people swipe away almost instantly if the opening does not grab them. The hook is the first one to three seconds: what you say, what text appears on screen and what the viewer sees. A good hook makes a clear promise or creates curiosity that the rest of the video pays off.

Weak hook: "Hi everyone, welcome back to my channel, today I want to talk about saving money."

Stronger hooks:

- "This one habit costs most salaried people in India thousands every year."
- "Stop paying for food delivery like this."
- "I tracked every rupee I spent for 30 days. Here's what shocked me."

Each one gets straight to the point. Note that the third must be true: only use "I tracked every rupee" if you actually did.

## A reliable short-video structure

For videos between 20 and 60 seconds, this structure works well:

| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 sec | Promise, surprise or problem |
| Setup | 3–10 sec | Why it matters, quick context |
| Value | 10–45 sec | The tips, steps, story or demonstration |
| Payoff | Last 5–10 sec | The key takeaway or reveal |
| Call to action | Final seconds | Follow, save, comment a keyword, or nothing |

Keep the pace brisk. Every sentence should either deliver value or make people want the next sentence.

## Common hook styles

Ask AI for hooks across different styles, then choose:

- **Problem**: "If your reels get under 200 views, check this first."
- **Mistake**: "You're probably storing your rotis wrong."
- **Curiosity**: "Nobody told me this before my first job interview."
- **Number**: "3 Excel shortcuts that saved me an hour a day."
- **Contrarian**: "You don't need a gym membership to get strong."
- **Story**: "A customer walked into our Jaipur shop and asked for a refund..."
- **Visual**: an unusual action on screen, such as pouring batter in slow motion, with text overlay.

## Writing the script with AI

Give the AI your topic, your audience, your voice guide and your raw knowledge. Ask for spoken language, not written language.

```text
Using my voice guide, write a 45-second Instagram Reel / YouTube Short
script about [topic] for [audience].
Requirements:
- Written to be spoken aloud: short sentences, natural rhythm.
- Roughly 110–130 spoken words.
- Format as a table: Time | What I say | On-screen text | Visual / B-roll.
- Structure: hook, setup, value, payoff, call to action.
- Use only the facts in my notes below.
- Then write 8 alternative hooks in different styles (problem, mistake,
  curiosity, number, contrarian, story, visual, question), each under
  12 words.
NOTES:
[paste your notes]
```

The "on-screen text" column matters because many people watch with the sound off. Captions and text overlays carry the message even on mute.

## Example: a Coimbatore fitness coach

Karthik coaches busy office workers. His notes: most clients skip workouts because they think 10 minutes is pointless; he recommends a simple routine; consistency matters more than intensity.

A slice of the AI-assisted script after his edits:

| Time | What I say | On-screen text | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | "Ten minutes of exercise is not a waste of time." | 10 minutes counts | Karthik checking his watch |
| 3–10s | "Most of my clients skip workouts because they can't do a full hour." | No time for a full workout? | Office desk, laptop closing |
| 10–40s | "Try this: two minutes of squats, two of push-ups..." | Step labels | Quick demo of each move |
| 40–45s | "Do it daily for two weeks. Then tell me it was pointless." | Try it for 14 days | Karthik smiling, thumbs up |

## Testing hook variants

You rarely know in advance which hook will work. Plan to test:

- Record the same video body once, then record three different hooks.
- Publish the versions on different days, or use any built-in testing features a platform offers.
- Compare how many people kept watching past the first few seconds (most platforms show retention or "skip rate" in their analytics) and how many watched to the end.

Over time you will learn which hook styles your audience responds to, and you can add those patterns to your voice guide.

## Recording tips that make AI scripts work

- **Rehearse aloud** and rewrite any line you trip over. AI scripts often contain phrases nobody says.
- **Use natural Hinglish or your regional language** if that is how your audience speaks (Lesson 3.4 covers this).
- **Film vertically**, in good natural light, with a simple clip-on mic if possible.
- **Add captions** with an auto-captioning tool and correct the errors; they are common with Indian names and mixed languages.

## Try it yourself

Complete Project 4 in about 20 minutes.

1. Pick a topic from your calendar that suits a quick demonstration or tip.
2. Write 100–200 words of raw notes.
3. Run the script prompt and get the table plus eight hooks.
4. Read the script aloud with a timer. Cut or rewrite until it fits 45 seconds and sounds like you.
5. Choose your three strongest hooks and write matching on-screen text for each.
6. Save the final script and the three hook variants in your calendar, with a note on how you will test them.

## Key takeaways

- The first few seconds decide whether a short video is watched; write hooks with care.
- Use the hook, setup, value, payoff, call to action structure for 20–60 second videos.
- Ask AI for scripts in a table with spoken words, on-screen text and visuals.
- Generate hooks in several styles and test variants with the same video body.
- Read every script aloud and make sure every claim in the hook is true.
