Visuals decide whether people stop scrolling. AI image tools can now produce illustrations, backgrounds and design elements in seconds. In this lesson you will learn the main categories of AI image tools, how to write good image prompts, and how to create thumbnails and post graphics that look professional and honest.

## The main categories of AI image tools

Tools change quickly, so focus on categories rather than specific versions:

| Category | Examples | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Image generation inside chat assistants | ChatGPT's image generation, Gemini and similar | Quick concepts, illustrations, editing by conversation |
| Design tools with AI features | Canva's AI features, Adobe Express and similar | Templates, layouts, background removal, resizing, text with images |
| Dedicated image generators | Midjourney-style tools, Adobe Firefly and others | Highly stylised art, detailed illustrations, consistent aesthetics |
| Photo editing AI | Background removal, object removal, upscaling, lighting fixes in phone and desktop apps | Improving real photos of you or your products |

For most creators, a design tool with templates plus one image generator is plenty. Many have free tiers with limits; check each tool's current plans and usage terms.

## When to use AI images and when not to

AI images work well for:

- Illustrations of concepts ("a tangled set of wires turning into a straight line" for a post on simplifying processes)
- Backgrounds and textures for carousels and quote cards
- Thumbnail elements such as icons, props or stylised scenes
- Mood boards and ideas before a real photoshoot

Use real photos instead when:

- **Showing your actual product**. A Pune bakery should never post an AI-generated cake as if it were theirs. That misleads customers.
- **Showing yourself**. Your face builds trust; AI-generated "you" can feel uncanny or deceptive.
- **Showing results, events or testimonials**. These must be genuine.
- **Showing real places or people** in a way that could mislead.

The test: would a reasonable viewer feel deceived if they learned the image was AI-generated? If yes, use a real photo or clearly label it.

## Writing good image prompts

A useful image prompt has five parts:

1. **Subject**: what is in the image.
2. **Setting**: where it is.
3. **Style**: photo, flat illustration, 3D, watercolour, minimal line art.
4. **Composition**: close-up, wide, centred, empty space on one side for text.
5. **Mood and colour**: warm, calm, energetic; your brand colours.

```text
Flat vector illustration of a young Indian woman working on a laptop
at a small desk with a cup of chai, plants in the background.
Calm, warm mood. Colour palette: deep teal, mustard yellow, off-white.
Plenty of empty space on the right side for headline text.
Square format. No text in the image.
```

Asking for "no text in the image" is useful because many generators still produce garbled lettering. Add your text in a design tool instead.

You can also ask a chat assistant to write image prompts for you: "Suggest 5 image prompts for a carousel cover about saving tax as a freelancer, in a flat illustration style using my brand colours."

## Designing YouTube thumbnails

Thumbnails are small on phones, so simplicity wins:

- **One focal point**: a face with clear expression, or a single object.
- **Three to five words of text at most**, large and high-contrast.
- **Complement the title**, do not repeat it.
- **Consistent style** across your channel so people recognise you.
- **Honesty**: the thumbnail must reflect what the video delivers. Misleading thumbnails can increase clicks briefly but hurt retention, trust and can violate platform policies.

A practical workflow: take a real photo of yourself with a clear expression, remove the background with an AI tool, place it on an AI-generated or simple background, then add bold text in a design tool. Create two or three variants; some platforms offer thumbnail testing features you can use to compare them.

## Instagram and LinkedIn graphics

For social posts, build a small set of reusable templates in a design tool: a quote card, a tip card, a carousel cover and a carousel content slide. Use AI for:

- Generating background illustrations that match your palette
- Resizing a design for different formats (square, portrait, story)
- Removing backgrounds from product photos
- Suggesting layout variations

Keep your brand consistent: same fonts, same two or three colours, similar illustration style.

## Quality and ethics checks

Before publishing any AI image, check:

- **Hands, faces, text and logos**: common sources of errors.
- **Cultural accuracy**: clothing, festivals, food and architecture often come out wrong or stereotyped. An "Indian wedding" image may mix traditions from different regions.
- **Real people and brands**: do not generate images of real people (celebrities, politicians, private individuals) or other companies' logos and trademarks.
- **Representation**: ask for the diversity that reflects your actual audience.
- **Platform labelling rules**: some platforms ask you to label realistic AI-generated or edited content. We cover this in Lesson 4.3.

## Try it yourself

Spend about 20 minutes creating visuals for your Project 3 carousel or a post.

1. Write a five-part image prompt for your carousel cover or a post illustration, using your brand colours and leaving space for text.
2. Generate at least four variations in any AI image tool you have access to.
3. Choose the best, check it for errors, and place it in a design tool template.
4. Add your headline text in the design tool, not in the generator.
5. If you make videos, create two thumbnail variants using a real photo of yourself plus a background.
6. Save your prompts in a "visual prompts" note so you can reproduce the style.

## Key takeaways

- Know the categories: chat-based generation, design tools with AI, dedicated generators and photo editing AI.
- Use AI for concepts, backgrounds and illustrations; use real photos for products, yourself and results.
- Write prompts with subject, setting, style, composition, mood and colour, and add text separately.
- Keep thumbnails simple, readable on phones and honest about the content.
- Check every image for errors, cultural accuracy, real-person issues and labelling requirements.
