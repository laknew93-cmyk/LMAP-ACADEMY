Your brand voice is how you sound: the words you choose, the rhythm of your sentences, the things you would never say. In this lesson you will use AI to analyse your existing writing and turn it into a one-page brand voice guide, the first project of this course.

## Why a voice guide matters even more with AI

When you ask an AI to "write a LinkedIn post about time management", it falls back on the most average version of a LinkedIn post. That is why so much AI content sounds the same. A voice guide fixes this. It is a short document you paste at the start of any AI conversation so the drafts start closer to how you actually speak.

It also helps when other people write for you. If you are a social media manager handling a dentist's clinic in Nagpur and a café in Kochi, a voice guide for each client stops their posts from blending into one another.

## Step 1: Collect your voice samples

AI cannot invent your voice from nothing. Gather 5–10 pieces of writing or speech that feel most like you:

- WhatsApp messages where you explained something to a friend or client
- Emails you were proud of
- Past posts that got good responses
- A transcript of a voice note or a talk (record yourself explaining your work for three minutes and use any transcription tool)

Spoken samples are often better than written ones. Many people write stiffly but speak naturally. If your writing feels formal, record yourself answering "What do you do and why does it matter?" and use the transcript.

Remove names, phone numbers and client details before pasting anything into an AI tool.

## Step 2: Ask AI to analyse, not imitate

The first prompt should ask for analysis, not new content:

```text
Below are samples of my writing and speech. Analyse my voice in detail:
1. Sentence length and rhythm
2. Vocabulary: words and phrases I use often; level of formality
3. Tone: e.g. warm, blunt, playful, calm
4. How I open and close messages
5. Use of humour, stories, data, or Hindi/regional words
6. Anything distinctive that a copywriter should preserve
Quote short examples from my samples to support each point.
Do not write any new content yet.

SAMPLES:
[paste samples here]
```

Read the analysis carefully. AI will sometimes flatter you or describe a voice you do not recognise. Push back: "You said my tone is inspirational, but I think it is practical and a bit sarcastic. Re-check the samples."

## Step 3: Define your "this, not that" pairs

The most useful part of any voice guide is a list of contrasts. They are clearer for both humans and AI than vague adjectives like "authentic" or "engaging".

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Direct and practical | Preachy or motivational-poster |
| Friendly, like a senior colleague | Over-familiar or full of slang |
| Confident about what we know | Arrogant or dismissive |
| Simple English, occasional Hindi phrase | Jargon-heavy corporate speak |

Ask the AI to propose six pairs based on its analysis, then rewrite them so each one feels true.

## Step 4: Build your banned and preferred word lists

Every voice has words it avoids. Here is a starter banned list that removes much of the generic AI flavour:

```text
Avoid: delve, unlock, unleash, game-changer, in today's fast-paced world,
elevate, seamless, journey (unless literal), leverage (as a verb),
"Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", excessive emojis, rhetorical
questions as openers.
```

Then add your own: phrases you dislike, competitor clichés, words that feel wrong for your audience. Add a preferred list too. A Pune home baker might prefer "fresh out of the oven" and "made to order", and avoid "artisanal" and "gourmet".

## Step 5: Assemble the one-page guide

Your finished guide should fit on one page so you can paste it anywhere. Use this template:

```text
BRAND VOICE GUIDE — [Name / Brand]

Who I am: [one line]
Brand promise: I help [who] with [what] so that [result].
Audience: [who reads this, what they care about, what they already know]

Voice in three words: [e.g. practical, warm, candid]

This, not that:
- [pair 1]
- [pair 2]
- ...

Writing rules:
- Sentence length: [e.g. mostly short; one idea per sentence]
- Point of view: [first person, "I" not "we"]
- Language: [e.g. simple English; Hindi words only where natural]
- Emojis: [e.g. maximum one per post, never in the first line]
- Always include: [e.g. one specific example or number from real work]

Banned words/phrases: [list]
Preferred words/phrases: [list]

Sample paragraph that sounds exactly like me:
[paste a real paragraph you wrote]
```

The sample paragraph is crucial. AI imitates examples far better than it follows descriptions.

## Step 6: Test the guide

A voice guide is only useful if it changes the output. Run a quick blind test:

1. In a new chat, ask for a 150-word post on a topic in your field without the guide.
2. In another new chat, paste the guide first, then ask for the same post.
3. Compare. Read both aloud. Which sounds more like you?

If the guided version still feels off, find the specific lines that sound wrong and add a rule that would have prevented them. Your guide should get a little sharper every few weeks.

## Try it yourself

Create Project 1, your brand voice guide, in about 20 minutes.

1. Collect at least five voice samples (one should be a transcript of you speaking).
2. Run the analysis prompt and correct anything that feels inaccurate.
3. Write six "this, not that" pairs and your banned and preferred word lists.
4. Fill in the one-page template, including a real sample paragraph and your brand promise from Lesson 1.
5. Run the blind test above and add at least one new rule based on what you notice.
6. Save it as "Brand Voice Guide v1" somewhere you can copy it from quickly, such as a notes app on your phone.

## Key takeaways

- A voice guide is a short, pasteable document that stops AI drafts from sounding generic.
- Start from real samples, especially spoken ones, and ask AI to analyse before it imitates.
- Concrete "this, not that" pairs and word lists beat vague adjectives.
- Always include a real paragraph you wrote; examples guide AI more than descriptions.
- Test the guide with a blind comparison and keep improving it.
