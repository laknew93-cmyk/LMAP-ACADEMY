Readers have become very good at spotting AI-generated content, and many scroll past it the moment they notice. This lesson shows you the common signs of "AI-sounding" writing and a practical editing process to make AI-assisted drafts sound unmistakably human and unmistakably you.

## Why AI drafts sound generic

Language models are trained to produce likely text. Left to themselves, they choose safe, familiar phrases and balanced, polished structures. The result is writing that is grammatically perfect but forgettable. The fix is not a special tool that claims to make text "undetectable". The fix is better inputs and honest editing.

## The tell-tale signs

Learn to spot these patterns in your drafts:

**Phrases and vocabulary**
- Grand openers: "In today's fast-paced digital landscape..."
- Overused words: delve, unlock, elevate, seamless, robust, game-changer, testament, tapestry, navigate (used metaphorically)
- Empty intensifiers: "truly", "incredibly", "absolutely essential"

**Structure**
- Every paragraph the same length
- Constant groups of three: "clarity, consistency and confidence"
- "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions
- Lists where prose would be more natural
- A neat moral at the end of every post

**Tone**
- Relentless positivity with no edge or opinion
- Hedging everything: "it may be worth considering"
- Generic engagement bait: "Agree? Let me know your thoughts below!"

**Content**
- No specific names, places, numbers or dates
- Claims nobody could disagree with
- Made-up statistics or vague references to "studies show"

None of these is wrong once. The problem is when they pile up.

## Fix it upstream: better inputs

Most generic output comes from generic input. Before you edit, improve what you feed the AI:

1. **Paste your voice guide** with its banned word list.
2. **Start from your own brain dump**, not a topic.
3. **Include specifics**: the client's industry, the city, the exact objection someone raised, the number you saw on your dashboard.
4. **Give it a real example** of your writing to imitate.
5. **Ask for a rough draft**, not a polished one. "Write this as a rough first draft, plainly, without trying to impress" often produces more natural text.

## Fix it downstream: the human edit

Use this five-pass edit on every piece. It takes five to ten minutes for a LinkedIn post.

### Pass 1: Delete the throat-clearing

Remove the first sentence if it is a general statement about the world. Remove the last line if it is generic engagement bait. Most drafts improve instantly.

### Pass 2: Add one thing only you could write

A specific detail is the strongest signal that a human with real experience wrote the post. For example:

- Generic: "Many small businesses struggle with cash flow."
- Specific: "Last March, a Ludhiana hosiery client had ₹18 lakh stuck with two distributors and couldn't pay salaries on time."

(If you use client stories, anonymise them and make sure you have permission where needed.)

### Pass 3: Take a position

Find the sentence where the draft sits on the fence and decide what you actually think. "There are pros and cons to hiring freelancers" becomes "For most early-stage startups, a good freelancer beats a mediocre full-time hire."

### Pass 4: Fix the rhythm

Read the post aloud. Break up long sentences. Vary paragraph lengths. Allow a one-word sentence if it fits. Replace any phrase you would not say to a colleague over chai.

### Pass 5: Check every fact

Verify numbers, dates, names, laws and quotes. Delete any statistic you cannot trace to a reliable source. If the AI added a claim you did not give it, remove it.

## Using AI to critique, not just write

AI is surprisingly useful as an editor of its own drafts when you ask it to be harsh:

```text
Act as a blunt editor who dislikes generic content. Review the post
below against my voice guide. List:
1. Every phrase that sounds generic, clichéd or AI-generated
2. Every claim that is vague or unsupported
3. Places where I should add a specific example from my experience
4. Sentences that hedge instead of taking a position
Do not rewrite the post. Just give me the list.
```

Asking it not to rewrite keeps you in control. You make the changes in your own words.

## A before and after

Before (raw AI draft):

```text
In today's competitive job market, networking is more important than
ever. Building genuine connections can unlock incredible opportunities
and elevate your career to new heights. Remember, your network is your
net worth! What are your top networking tips? Share below!
```

After (human edit):

```text
I got my last two jobs without applying.

Both came from people I'd helped years earlier: once by reviewing a
friend's pitch deck, once by answering a stranger's question about
moving from Infosys to a startup.

Networking isn't collecting contacts. It's being useful before you
need anything.
```

The edited version has a specific claim, real details and a clear opinion. It is shorter and far more believable.

## A note on AI detectors

Some people run their content through "AI detectors" before posting. These tools are unreliable: they can flag human writing as AI and miss edited AI text. Do not build your workflow around passing detectors. Build it around being specific, accurate and genuinely useful.

## Try it yourself

Spend 15 minutes on this editing drill.

1. Take one of the LinkedIn posts you drafted for Project 3.
2. Run the blunt editor prompt and read the list it returns.
3. Apply the five passes yourself: delete throat-clearing, add one unique detail, take a position, fix rhythm, check facts.
4. Compare word counts before and after. Most good edits make the post shorter.
5. Add any new banned phrases you noticed to your brand voice guide.

## Key takeaways

- AI drafts sound generic because models default to safe, likely phrasing.
- Learn the signs: grand openers, overused words, rigid structure, no specifics, no opinion.
- Improve inputs with your voice guide, brain dumps and real details.
- Edit in five passes: cut, add specifics, take a position, fix rhythm, check facts.
- Use AI as a harsh critic, and ignore unreliable AI detectors.
