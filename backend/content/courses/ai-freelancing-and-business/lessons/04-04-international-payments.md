Working with clients in the US, UK, UAE, Australia or Europe opens up a bigger market, but getting paid across borders brings new questions: which platform to use, how much you lose in fees, and what paperwork matters. This lesson explains the main options in plain terms.

**Note:** Fees, limits and features of payment services change frequently. Always check each provider's current terms. For tax and compliance questions, consult a CA.

## How international payments generally work

When a foreign client pays you, money moves from their currency (say USD) into Indian rupees in your bank account. Along the way, there are usually:

- **Transfer or platform fees** — charged by the payment service.
- **Currency conversion** — the rate you receive may differ from the rate you see on Google; the difference is effectively a cost.
- **Bank charges** — some banks charge for receiving foreign inward remittances.

Compare the **final rupee amount** you receive, not just the headline fee.

## Main ways to receive payment

### 1. Through freelance platforms

If you work on Upwork or Fiverr, clients pay the platform and you withdraw your earnings to your Indian bank account or another supported method. Benefits: payment protection rules, no need to chase invoices. Costs: platform service fees plus withdrawal or conversion charges.

### 2. Direct bank transfer (wire / SWIFT)

The client sends money directly to your Indian bank account using your bank details and SWIFT code. This is straightforward for larger amounts, but intermediary and receiving charges can apply and it can take a few days.

### 3. Payment and collection services

Several services let you receive international payments through local account details in the client's country or via invoices they can pay by card or bank transfer. Commonly used options among Indian freelancers include PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, and India-focused export payment services such as Skydo. Each has different fee structures, speeds and documentation. Compare them on:

| Factor | What to check |
|---|---|
| Fees | Fixed fee, percentage, and conversion margin |
| Speed | How long until rupees reach your account |
| Client convenience | Can the client pay easily by their preferred method? |
| Documentation | Do you receive proper proof of inward remittance? |
| Limits | Any per-transaction or monthly limits |
| Support | Ease of resolving issues |

You do not need all of them. Start with one or two that suit your clients.

## Proof of payment: why it matters

For foreign income, keep proper documentation showing money came into India from abroad for services. Banks and payment services typically provide documents such as a FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate) or an equivalent advice. These can be important for:

- Showing that your service qualifies as an export (relevant for GST — see the previous lesson).
- Your income tax records.
- Answering any bank queries.

Your bank may ask for the **purpose of remittance** when money arrives. Choose the purpose that accurately describes your service; if unsure, ask your bank or CA.

## Invoicing international clients

- Quote and invoice in the client's currency (commonly USD, GBP, EUR, AUD or AED).
- Include your full name, address, and the client's full details.
- Describe services clearly.
- State payment method and terms.
- If you are GST-registered and exporting under LUT, your CA can tell you what wording the invoice needs.

## Protecting yourself with international clients

International work has its own risks:

- **Take an advance** for direct (off-platform) clients, especially new ones.
- **Use milestones** for longer projects: pay per stage completed.
- **Be wary of overpayment scams**: a "client" sends more than the invoice amount and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment often turns out to be fraudulent and gets reversed.
- **Never share login credentials** of your bank or payment accounts.
- **Avoid unusual payment methods** like gift cards or crypto from unknown clients.
- **Keep platform clients on the platform** until there's a clear, legitimate reason to move, and check platform rules first — many platforms prohibit taking clients off-platform for a period.

## Handling time zones and communication

Payment is easier when the relationship is smooth:

- State your working hours in the client's time zone.
- Use shared calendars for calls.
- Send short end-of-day updates when working on active projects.
- Use AI to polish important messages, but keep them natural:

```text
Rewrite this message to a US client in clear, friendly, professional
English. Keep it short and keep my meaning exactly the same:
[your message]
```

## Try it yourself

About 15 minutes.

1. Check whether your bank account can receive international payments and what documents it provides for inward remittances. Most bank websites have an FAQ on this.
2. Choose two payment services and note their current fees, speed and documentation from their official websites.
3. Calculate how many rupees you would actually receive for an example $200 payment through each option.
4. Update your invoice template with a version for international clients.
5. Write a short "payment terms" paragraph for international proposals (advance, milestones, method).

## Key takeaways

- Compare final rupee amounts, accounting for fees and conversion margins.
- Main options: platform withdrawals, bank wire, and international payment services.
- Keep proof of inward remittance (such as a FIRC) for GST and income tax purposes.
- Protect yourself with advances, milestones, and awareness of overpayment scams.
