"You need experience to get clients, and clients to get experience." Every beginner meets this loop. A small, focused portfolio breaks it. In this lesson you will build Project 2: a three-piece portfolio, created quickly with AI's help but honestly presented.

## What a portfolio really needs to do

A client looking at your portfolio is asking three questions:

1. Can this person do the kind of work I need?
2. Do they understand businesses like mine?
3. Will working with them be easy?

Three well-chosen pieces that answer these questions beat thirty random samples.

## Three ways to get portfolio material

### 1. Spec work (self-initiated samples)

Create work for a realistic, imagined brief. For example: "A one-month Instagram plan for a fictional organic grocery store in Coimbatore." Label it clearly as a **sample project**. Do not use a real company's logo or name as if they hired you.

### 2. Redesign or improvement samples

Pick a public example — a cluttered restaurant menu, a confusing FAQ page, a dull company profile — and show your improved version. Again, label it as an independent sample, avoid reproducing their full copyrighted text, and do not present it as client work. A "before and after" structure is powerful because it shows your thinking.

### 3. Real work for real people

Offer a small, clearly defined project to someone in your network: your uncle's hardware shop, a friend's home tuition class, an NGO you volunteer with. You can do it at a reduced rate or free in exchange for a testimonial and permission to show the work. Keep the scope small so it does not drag on.

A mix works well: one or two spec samples plus one real project.

## Building each piece with AI — step by step

Let us say your niche is social media for home bakers.

**Step 1: Write a realistic brief.** Use AI to help you create one, then edit it.

```text
Create a realistic client brief for a home baker in Pune who sells
custom cakes and cookies through Instagram and WhatsApp. Include:
business background, target customers, current problems, goals for
next month, brand tone, and constraints (budget, time, photos available).
Keep it under 250 words.
```

**Step 2: Plan before producing.** Ask for a content calendar outline, then decide the final plan yourself — choose festivals and local moments that make sense (Ganesh Chaturthi modak-flavoured cookies, for instance).

**Step 3: Produce the work.** Draft captions with AI, design posts in a design tool using free-for-commercial-use elements, and edit everything through the quality workflow from the previous lesson.

**Step 4: Write a short case study.** This is the part most beginners skip, and it is what makes clients trust you.

## The mini case-study format

For each piece, write 150–250 words:

```text
Project: [Name] (Sample project / Client project)
Client type: [e.g. home bakery, Pune]
Challenge: [What problem the work addresses]
What I did: [3-5 bullet points]
How I used AI: [e.g. idea generation and first-draft captions;
all copy edited and designs created by me]
Result: [For real projects: honest outcome or client feedback.
For samples: what the client would receive and why it works]
```

Notice "Result". For spec work, do not invent numbers like "increased engagement by 40%". It did not happen, and a client who discovers this will never trust you again. Explain your reasoning instead.

## Choosing your three pieces

Your three pieces should show range *within* your niche, not across random services:

| Niche | Piece 1 | Piece 2 | Piece 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content for CA firms | Blog post on a tax-filing topic | 5 LinkedIn posts for a partner | Client newsletter |
| Data cleaning for distributors | Cleaned customer list (anonymised) | Sales summary dashboard | Before/after duplicate removal |
| Subtitles for Hindi educators | Subtitled 3-minute clip | Glossary of technical terms | Translated video description |
| Chatbot setup for clinics | FAQ document | WhatsApp quick-reply set | Test log showing bot responses |

For data work, always use dummy or fully anonymised data. Never show a real client's customer list.

## Where to host your portfolio

You do not need a website on day one. Options:

- A neat **Google Drive or Notion page** with a short intro and links to each piece.
- A **PDF portfolio** of five to eight pages — easy to share on WhatsApp and email.
- **LinkedIn Featured section** — pin your best pieces.
- Platform portfolios on **Upwork** or **Fiverr**, which we cover in the next lessons.
- An **Instagram profile** if your niche is visual.

Make sure links open without permission requests. Test them from a different account or an incognito window.

## Ethics checklist

Before publishing, confirm:

- Every sample is clearly labelled as a sample if it was not paid client work.
- No real brand logos or trademarks are used to imply a relationship.
- No invented metrics, testimonials or client names.
- Any real client has given permission to show the work.
- Your AI use is described honestly.

## Try it yourself

This is a larger project, so do it in parts. The first part takes about 20 minutes.

1. Decide your three portfolio pieces using the table above as a guide.
2. For the first piece, use AI to generate a realistic brief and edit it until it feels true to life.
3. Write the case-study skeleton (Project, Client type, Challenge) before producing the work. This keeps you focused.
4. Block two or three sessions in your calendar this week to complete all three pieces.
5. Message one person in your network offering a small, clearly scoped project for a testimonial.

## Key takeaways

- Three focused pieces within your niche are enough to start winning work.
- Mix spec samples, improvement samples and at least one real project if possible.
- A short case study for each piece shows your thinking and builds trust.
- Label samples honestly, never invent results, and anonymise any data.
