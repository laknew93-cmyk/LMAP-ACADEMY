Winning a client is the start, not the finish. Repeat work, referrals and testimonials all come from how you deliver. This lesson covers the delivery process from kickoff to handover, with AI helping at each stage and you staying firmly in charge of quality.

## The delivery lifecycle

Every project, big or small, follows roughly the same path:

1. **Kickoff** — understand the brief and collect inputs.
2. **Plan** — agree on what will be delivered and when.
3. **Produce** — create the work.
4. **Quality check** — review, verify, polish.
5. **Deliver** — hand over clearly.
6. **Revise** — handle feedback within agreed limits.
7. **Close** — final files, invoice, testimonial request.

## Kickoff: ask better questions

Most project problems start with a misunderstood brief. A short kickoff call or questionnaire prevents this. AI can help you prepare:

```text
I'm starting a [service] project for [client type]. Create a kickoff
questionnaire of 10-12 questions covering goals, audience, tone,
examples they like and dislike, must-include facts, deadlines,
approval process, and who gives final sign-off.
```

Edit the list, then send it via Google Form or go through it on a call. Record answers in a single brief document that you both can see.

Always confirm:

- Who gives final approval? (In family businesses, it may be someone other than the person you speak to.)
- What does "done" look like?
- What facts must be exactly right — prices, addresses, dates, product specs?

## Plan: confirm in writing

After kickoff, send a short summary:

```text
Hi Meena, summarising today's call:
- Deliverables: 12 Instagram posts for November
- Inputs from you: product photos by 3 Nov
- First batch (6 posts) by 7 Nov, second batch by 14 Nov
- 1 round of revisions per batch
- Approval: you and Rajesh ji
Please reply "confirmed" or let me know any changes.
```

This message protects both of you and becomes a reference if confusion arises later.

## Produce: AI-assisted, human-led

Use the prompts and templates you have built. A few service-specific tips:

- **Writing:** give the AI the client's own words from the kickoff — phrases they use, stories they shared. This reduces generic output.
- **Social media:** design in batches, and keep a brand kit (colours, fonts, logo) consistent.
- **Data cleaning:** always work on a copy. Document each change (removed duplicates, standardised phone formats) in a "change log" sheet.
- **Transcription and translation:** review with the original audio playing; flag unclear sections rather than guessing.
- **Chatbots/FAQs:** test every answer against the owner's confirmed information. Try confusing or unexpected questions to see how it responds.
- **Automation:** test with sample data, including edge cases like blank fields.

## Quality check: your pre-delivery checklist

Create a checklist for your service and use it every time. An example for written content:

```text
[ ] Matches brief: audience, tone, length, format
[ ] All facts, numbers, names verified against source or client
[ ] No invented quotes, statistics or references
[ ] Brand-specific details included (not generic)
[ ] Spelling and grammar checked; Indian vs US English as requested
[ ] Originality check done for long-form pieces
[ ] Links work; images are licensed for commercial use
[ ] Read once fully, aloud if possible
```

AI can act as a second reviewer:

```text
Review this draft against the brief below. List: (1) anything in the
brief that is missing, (2) any statements that sound like they need
fact-checking, (3) generic phrases that could be more specific.
Do not rewrite - just list issues.
```

You still make the final judgement; the AI may miss issues or flag non-issues.

## Deliver: make it easy to approve

- Send files in the format agreed, with clear names: `MeenaBoutique_Nov_Posts_Batch1.pdf`, not `final_final2.pdf`.
- Include a short note on what's included and what you need from them.
- For data or automation work, add a simple "how to use this" guide.

## Revisions: stay calm and clear

Feedback is normal. Handle it professionally:

- Thank them and confirm your understanding of each change.
- If feedback is vague ("make it pop"), ask for an example.
- If requests go beyond scope, say so politely and offer a quote:

```text
Happy to make these two caption changes. Adding reels scripts would be
outside this month's package - I can add 4 scripts for ₹[amount]
if you'd like.
```

## Close: finish strongly

- Deliver final files and any source files agreed in the contract.
- Send the invoice (covered in Lesson 4.3).
- Ask for feedback and a testimonial while the project is fresh:

```text
It was a pleasure working with you. If you were happy with the work,
would you mind writing 2-3 lines I could share as a testimonial?
And if you know anyone who might need similar help, I'd be grateful
for an introduction.
```

## Communicate proactively

Clients rarely mind small delays if you tell them early. They mind silence. If you will miss a deadline, inform them before it passes, explain briefly, and give a new date. Set expectations about your working hours and response time too — for example, "I reply within one working day."

## Try it yourself

About 15–20 minutes.

1. Use the AI prompt to create a kickoff questionnaire for your service. Edit it to 10 questions.
2. Write a pre-delivery quality checklist for your service with at least eight items.
3. Draft your "plan confirmation" message template and your "close and testimonial" message template.
4. Save all of these in one folder named "Delivery kit". You will turn them into a full SOP in Module 5.

## Key takeaways

- Follow a clear lifecycle: kickoff, plan, produce, quality check, deliver, revise, close.
- Confirm scope, timelines and approvers in writing.
- Use a service-specific checklist every time; AI can be a second reviewer, not the final one.
- Handle revisions calmly, quote for out-of-scope work, and ask for testimonials at the close.
