Getting paid on time and staying on the right side of tax rules are part of running a real freelance business. This lesson covers professional invoicing, GST basics and income tax awareness for freelancers in India — in plain language.

**Important:** This is general information to help you ask the right questions. Tax rules, thresholds and rates change, and your situation depends on many factors. Please consult a Chartered Accountant (CA) before making tax or GST decisions, and check the official GST and Income Tax portals for current rules.

## Professional invoicing

Every payment should have an invoice. It makes you look professional, helps clients with their own accounting, and gives you clean records for tax filing.

### What an invoice should include

- Your name or business name, address, contact details
- Your PAN, and GSTIN if registered
- Client's name/business name and address (and their GSTIN if they have one and you are registered)
- Unique invoice number (e.g. INV-2026-014) and invoice date
- Description of services, quantity, rate, and amount
- Tax details if applicable
- Total amount due, due date, and payment details (bank account, UPI ID)
- For international clients: amount in the agreed currency

You can create invoices in Google Docs or Sheets, or use invoicing software. Many accounting apps used by Indian small businesses can generate GST-compliant invoices.

## GST basics for freelancers

GST (Goods and Services Tax) applies to the supply of services, including freelance work. Key ideas to discuss with your CA:

### Registration threshold

Service providers generally need to register for GST once their aggregate annual turnover crosses a threshold — commonly ₹20 lakh for most states, and lower (₹10 lakh) for certain special category states. There are situations where registration may be required regardless of turnover, so confirm with a CA how the rules apply to you — for example, if you sell through certain e-commerce operators or have specific types of clients.

### Voluntary registration

Some freelancers register voluntarily below the threshold, for instance because certain business clients prefer GST-registered vendors. Registration brings compliance work — regular returns and record-keeping — so weigh the pros and cons with your CA.

### GST rate

Many professional and freelance services fall under an 18% GST rate, but classification matters. Confirm the right rate for your service.

### Export of services

Services provided to clients outside India, paid in foreign currency, may qualify as an "export of services". Exports are generally treated as zero-rated, and registered exporters commonly file a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) to export without paying IGST upfront. The conditions are specific, so get professional guidance.

## Income tax basics

Freelance earnings are taxable income. Points to understand and discuss with your CA:

- **Records:** keep track of all income received and business expenses (laptop, internet, software subscriptions, a portion of phone bills where justified).
- **ITR form:** freelancers typically file under income from business or profession, which uses a different ITR form than salaried individuals.
- **Presumptive taxation:** the Income Tax Act has presumptive schemes (such as Section 44ADA for certain professions and 44AD for businesses) that simplify computation for eligible taxpayers. Whether they apply depends on your type of work and turnover — ask your CA.
- **TDS:** Indian business clients may deduct TDS (tax deducted at source) before paying you. This shows up in your Form 26AS/AIS, and you can claim credit for it when filing.
- **Advance tax:** if your tax liability for the year crosses a certain amount, you may need to pay advance tax in instalments rather than all at year end.
- **Side income alongside a job:** freelance income is added to your salary income for the year; your employer's TDS won't cover it automatically.

## Simple money habits from day one

1. **Open a separate bank account** for freelance income and expenses. This makes records much easier.
2. **Set aside a percentage** of every payment for taxes in a separate savings account — your CA can help you choose a suitable percentage.
3. **Keep a simple income and expense sheet** with date, client, invoice number, amount, tax, and payment status.
4. **Save invoices and receipts** in monthly folders.
5. **Book a CA consultation** once you start earning regularly — it's a worthwhile cost that can prevent expensive mistakes.

## Questions to ask your CA

```text
1. Do I need to register for GST now, and should I register voluntarily?
2. What GST rate applies to my service?
3. Do my international clients count as export of services? Should I file an LUT?
4. Which ITR form should I use? Does a presumptive scheme apply to me?
5. Do I need to pay advance tax?
6. Which expenses can I legitimately claim?
7. What records should I maintain?
```

3. Generate your three payment reminder messages and save them in your "Delivery kit".

## Key takeaways

- Issue a proper invoice for every payment and keep organised records.
- GST registration depends on turnover thresholds and other conditions; export of services has special treatment.
- Freelance income is taxable; understand ITR forms, TDS, advance tax and presumptive schemes with a CA.
- Keep business money separate and set aside money for taxes from every payment.
