Not every "AI service" is something a real client will pay for. In this lesson we walk through seven AI-assisted services that solve everyday problems for Indian businesses and individuals, what each one actually involves, and where AI helps versus where your judgement is essential.

## The test: does it solve a painful, recurring problem?

Before you look at the list, keep one question in mind: *what problem does this solve, and who feels it every week?* A dentist in Indore who has no time to post on Instagram has a recurring problem. A startup founder who needs an investor deck by Friday has an urgent one. Services that solve recurring or urgent problems are easier to sell than "nice to have" ones.

## Seven services worth considering

### 1. Content writing

Blog posts, website pages, newsletters, product descriptions, LinkedIn posts for founders. AI speeds up research summaries, outlines and first drafts. Your value lies in interviewing the client, adding real examples, fact-checking, and making the writing sound like a person rather than a template.

**Typical clients:** CA firms wanting monthly tax-tip blogs, D2C brands needing product pages, coaches wanting LinkedIn ghostwriting.

### 2. Social media management

Planning a content calendar, writing captions, designing simple posts in a design tool, scheduling, and replying to comments. AI helps with ideas, captions, hashtags and resizing designs. You bring consistency, brand voice and local insight — knowing that a Kochi restaurant should post about Onam, not just generic "foodie Friday" content.

**Typical clients:** cafés, salons, boutiques, coaching centres, real estate agents.

### 3. AI chatbot and FAQ setup

Many small businesses get the same twenty questions on WhatsApp and Instagram every day: timings, prices, location, delivery areas. You can help them set up quick replies, greeting messages, a well-written FAQ, and — where it fits — a simple no-code chatbot on their website. The real work is collecting accurate answers from the owner and testing the bot so it never gives wrong information.

**Typical clients:** clinics, schools, travel agents, online sellers.

### 4. Data cleaning and spreadsheet work

Messy customer lists, duplicate entries, inconsistent phone number formats, sales data that needs a summary. AI assistants can suggest formulas, explain pivot tables, and write small scripts. You verify the output carefully, because one wrong formula across 3,000 rows is a serious mistake.

**Typical clients:** distributors, small manufacturers, NGOs, event organisers.

### 5. Presentations and documents

Pitch decks, training slides, company profiles, reports. AI helps structure content and draft slide text; design tools help with layouts. Your job is to make the story clear and the numbers correct.

**Typical clients:** startup founders, corporate trainers, consultants, college faculty.

### 6. Transcription, subtitles and translation

Converting interviews, podcasts and webinars into text; adding subtitles to videos; translating between English and Indian languages. Speech-to-text tools are now quite good, but accents, code-mixing ("Hinglish") and technical terms still cause errors. Human review is what clients pay for. Translation especially needs a fluent speaker to check tone and meaning.

**Typical clients:** YouTubers, ed-tech creators, researchers, NGOs producing material in regional languages.

### 7. Basic automation

Connecting tools so routine tasks happen on their own: a form submission goes into a Google Sheet and triggers a confirmation email; new orders get logged automatically. No-code automation platforms make this possible without programming. AI can help you design the flow and troubleshoot it.

**Typical clients:** small agencies, coaches, online course sellers, any business drowning in copy-paste work.

## Comparing the options

| Service | Starting skill needed | Ongoing (retainer) potential | Main risk to manage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content writing | Good written English or regional language | High | Generic or inaccurate content |
| Social media | Basic design sense, consistency | High | Missing posting schedules |
| Chatbot / FAQ setup | Patience, logical thinking | Medium | Bot giving wrong answers |
| Data cleaning | Comfort with Excel/Sheets | Medium | Silent errors in data |
| Presentations | Structure and visual sense | Low–medium | Wrong numbers, cluttered slides |
| Transcription / translation | Language fluency | Medium | Mistranslation, missed words |
| Basic automation | Logical thinking, tool curiosity | Medium | Broken flows nobody notices |

"Retainer potential" means how likely it is that a client pays you every month rather than once. Retainers make income steadier, so services like social media and content writing are popular starting points.

## Services to be careful with

Some things are sold online as "AI services" but carry real risk:

- **Writing college assignments or exam answers for students.** This is academic dishonesty. Do not offer it.
- **Fake reviews or fake followers.** Unethical and often against platform rules.
- **Legal, medical or financial advice content without expert review.** You can write *about* these topics only if a qualified professional reviews the content.
- **Deepfake or voice-clone work** using someone's likeness without clear consent.

Saying no to these protects your reputation, which is your most valuable asset as a freelancer.

## Try it yourself

Take 15–20 minutes.

1. From the seven services, pick the **three** that best match the skills you listed in Lesson 1.
2. For each, write one sentence describing a specific client and their problem, e.g. "A Nagpur physiotherapy clinic that gets 30 WhatsApp messages a day asking about timings and fees."
3. Use an AI assistant to stress-test your ideas:

```text
I am considering offering [service] to [type of client] in India.
Act as a sceptical small business owner. Give me 5 honest reasons
you would NOT hire a freelancer for this, and what would change your mind.
```

4. Note the objections. They are gold — they tell you what your offer must address.
5. Rank your three services from most to least promising based on this exercise.

## Key takeaways

- Sell solutions to painful, recurring or urgent problems, not "AI" as a buzzword.
- Seven practical services: content, social media, chatbots/FAQs, data cleaning, presentations, transcription/translation, and basic automation.
- In every service, AI does the first draft; human review is what makes it worth paying for.
- Refuse unethical work such as assignment writing, fake reviews or non-consensual deepfakes.
