Cold outreach means contacting people who have not asked to hear from you. Done badly, it is spam. Done well, it is a helpful, respectful note that starts a conversation. In this lesson you will write ten genuinely personalised outreach messages — the second half of Project 3.

## The principles of respectful outreach

1. **Research first.** Spend a few minutes understanding the business before you write.
2. **Lead with something useful.** An observation, a small idea, or a free tip — not a sales pitch.
3. **Keep it short.** Three to six sentences. Busy people skim.
4. **Make one small ask.** "Would it help if I sent two sample captions?" is easier to accept than "Shall we get on a call to discuss a retainer?"
5. **Respect "no" and silence.** Follow up once or twice at most, then stop.
6. **Use appropriate channels.** LinkedIn messages, business email addresses, and Instagram DMs to business accounts are generally acceptable. Bulk WhatsApp messages to strangers are not.

## Anatomy of a good outreach message

```text
[Personal observation] I noticed [specific thing about their business].
[Relevance] I help [niche] with [service].
[Value] One idea: [specific, useful suggestion].
[Small ask] Would you like me to [small, low-risk offer]?
[Easy exit] No worries if the timing isn't right.
```

### Example: LinkedIn, CA firm in Chennai

```text
Hi Ramesh, I read your post on new TDS changes - it was clear and
practical. I write blogs and LinkedIn content for CA firms. One idea:
your website FAQ could answer the three questions you mentioned
clients keep asking, which may save your team calls. Would a short
sample FAQ draft be useful? Happy either way.
```

### Example: Instagram DM, café in Indore

```text
Hi! Love the new cold coffee menu - the photos look great. I noticed
the last few posts don't mention your timings or location, which
customers often look for. I manage Instagram for local cafés. Would
you like 2 free sample captions for your next posts? No obligation.
```

### Example: Email, international e-commerce store

```text
Subject: Quick idea for your product pages

Hi Sarah,

I was browsing your handmade candle store - lovely range. A few product
pages have one-line descriptions, which may leave buyers unsure about
burn time and scent strength. I write product descriptions for small
online stores. Would you like me to rewrite one page as a free sample?

Best,
Arjun
```

Notice what these do: they mention something real, offer a concrete idea, and ask for something small.

## Using AI for research and personalisation

AI can help you go from research notes to a first draft quickly. The research itself — visiting their profile, reading their posts — you must do, because AI cannot reliably know the current state of a specific business.

**Step 1: Collect notes.** For each prospect, write 3–5 bullet points: what they sell, something you liked, a gap or opportunity you noticed, and their likely priority.

**Step 2: Draft.**

```text
Write a cold outreach message for [channel] using my notes below.
Structure: personal observation, relevance, one specific useful idea,
small ask, easy exit. Under 80 words. Warm, plain English. No flattery
that sounds fake, no "I hope this finds you well", no claims about
results I can't guarantee.

About me: [one-line niche statement]
Prospect notes: [your bullet points]
```

**Step 3: Edit.** Make sure the observation is accurate and in your voice. Shorten it further if you can.

Never send AI drafts without reading them. One message referencing the wrong product or business name ruins your credibility.

## Follow-ups

Many replies come after a follow-up, because people are busy, not uninterested. A simple sequence:

- **Day 0:** First message.
- **Day 4–5:** Short follow-up adding one new piece of value.
- **Day 10–14:** Final, gracious note.

```text
Hi Ramesh, just following up in case this got buried. I've drafted a
sample FAQ answer on the TDS question - happy to share if useful.
```

```text
Hi Ramesh, I won't keep messaging - if content support is ever useful
for your firm, I'm here. Wishing you a smooth filing season!
```

Then stop. Mark them "closed" in your tracker and move on.

## What to expect

Reply rates vary a lot with niche, channel and message quality. Many messages will go unanswered, and that is normal. Track your numbers so you can improve: if nobody replies, rework your observation and offer; if people reply but do not buy, revisit your pricing or proposal.

## Try it yourself

About 20 minutes for the first five messages; repeat for the next five.

1. From your tracker sheet (Lesson 3.1), pick 10 prospects that fit your niche.
2. For each, spend 2–3 minutes researching and write 3–5 bullet points of notes.
3. Use the AI prompt to create first drafts.
4. Edit each one by hand. Check the business name, the observation and the offer.
5. Read all ten side by side. If any two are nearly identical apart from the name, personalise them more.
6. Save them in a document titled "Outreach batch 1". Together with your proposal template, this completes **Project 3**.
7. Send them over the coming week as part of your routine, and log each in your tracker.

## Key takeaways

- Respectful outreach is researched, short, useful and makes one small ask.
- Do the research yourself; use AI to turn your notes into a draft and edit it carefully.
- Follow up at most twice, then stop gracefully.
- Track replies and outcomes so you can improve your message over time.
