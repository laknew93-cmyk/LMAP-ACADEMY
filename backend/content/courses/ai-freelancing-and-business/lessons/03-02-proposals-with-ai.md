A good proposal makes the client feel understood within the first two lines. A bad one — usually a generic, AI-generated wall of text — gets ignored. In this lesson you will build a reusable proposal template and learn to personalise it quickly with AI, without sounding like a robot.

## Why most proposals fail

Clients on Upwork or LinkedIn often receive many responses. They skim. Proposals that fail usually:

- Start with "Dear Sir/Madam, I am a hardworking freelancer with excellent skills…"
- Talk only about the freelancer, not the client's problem.
- Are obviously copy-pasted or obviously AI-written: long, generic, full of phrases like "I hope this message finds you well."
- Do not answer the questions in the job post.

## The structure of a strong proposal

Keep it short — often 120–200 words for platform proposals.

### 1. A hook that mirrors their problem

Show you read the brief. Reference a specific detail.

> "You mentioned your customer list has duplicates from three different sources and inconsistent phone formats — I've cleaned similar lists and can standardise everything to a single format."

### 2. Your approach in 2–4 steps

Clients want to know how you will work, not just that you can.

### 3. Relevant proof

One or two portfolio links with a line on why they are relevant.

### 4. Logistics

Timeline, what you need from them, and price (or a question that leads to it).

### 5. A simple next step

A question or call to action: "Could you share a 20-row sample so I can confirm the timeline?"

## Your reusable proposal template

```text
Hi [Name],

[Hook: one or two sentences referencing a specific detail from their
post or business, and the outcome they want.]

Here's how I'd approach it:
1. [Step 1]
2. [Step 2]
3. [Step 3 - including review/quality check]

Relevant work: [Portfolio link] - [one line on why it's similar].

Timeline: [X days] after I receive [inputs needed].
Price: [₹/$ amount or package name], including [revisions].

I use AI tools for speed, and every deliverable is reviewed and
checked by me personally.

[Question or next step]

[Your name]
```

Keep a few variations saved: one for platform jobs, one for direct LinkedIn or email leads, and one for local businesses (who may prefer a WhatsApp-friendly, shorter version).

## Personalising with AI — the right way

AI can save you time by analysing the brief and drafting a tailored version. The key is to feed it real information and keep control.

**Step 1: Analyse the brief.**

```text
Here is a job post. Summarise in bullet points:
- The client's main problem
- Their stated requirements
- Questions they asked that I must answer
- Any hidden concerns (budget, timeline, past bad experiences)
- Red flags, if any

[Paste job post]
```

**Step 2: Draft using your template.**

```text
Using my proposal template below, draft a proposal for this job.
Rules: under 170 words, plain friendly English, no "I hope this finds
you well", no exaggerated claims, do not invent experience or results.
Use only these portfolio pieces: [list with links and descriptions].

[Template]
[Job post]
```

**Step 3: Edit by hand.** Read it aloud. Rewrite the hook in your own words. Check every claim is true. Remove anything a human would not naturally write.

## Answering screening questions

Many job posts include questions. Answer each one directly, in the same order. If a post says "Start your proposal with the word 'mango' so I know you read this," do it — clients use this to filter copy-paste proposals.

## Proposals for local businesses

A Lucknow sweet shop owner will not read a formal proposal. Send a short, friendly WhatsApp or printed one-page version:

```text
Namaste [Name] ji,

Thank you for your time today. As discussed, here is what I can do
for [Shop name]'s Instagram:

- 12 posts a month (photos you share + my designs and captions)
- A festival calendar so you never miss Diwali, Raksha Bandhan etc.
- One short monthly update on what's working

Price: ₹[amount] per month. First month as a trial, no long commitment.

Shall I prepare 2 sample posts so you can see the style?
```

## Watch for red flags in briefs

Your analysis prompt may surface these, but learn to spot them yourself:

- Requests for "free sample work" that is actually the full project.
- Clients who want to move off-platform immediately or pay outside the platform's system.
- Unclear scope with a tiny fixed budget.
- Requests to pay a "registration" or "training" fee to get the job.

## Try it yourself

About 20 minutes. This is part one of Project 3.

1. Copy the proposal template into a document and customise it for your niche and service. This is your **proposal template** deliverable.
2. Find two real job posts (on Upwork, LinkedIn or elsewhere) that match your niche. You do not have to submit.
3. For each, run the brief-analysis prompt.
4. Draft a proposal using AI with your template, then edit it by hand. Track how many lines you changed.
5. Compare your two proposals. Do they sound different and specific to each client? If they look nearly identical, personalise further.

## Key takeaways

- Strong proposals are short, specific, and start with the client's problem.
- Use a five-part structure: hook, approach, proof, logistics, next step.
- Use AI to analyse briefs and draft, but write the hook yourself and verify every claim.
- Adapt the format to the client — a WhatsApp-friendly version works better for many local businesses.
