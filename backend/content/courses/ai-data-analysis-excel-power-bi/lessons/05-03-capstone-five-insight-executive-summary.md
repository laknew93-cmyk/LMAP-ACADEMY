Numbers don't change decisions; clear sentences do. In this final project you'll learn to ask sharper analytical questions, turn findings into a story, and write a one-page, five-insight executive summary that a busy director will actually read.

## From "what happened" to "so what"

Most reports stop at description: "Sales were ₹51.0 lakh in September." Useful, but the reader still has to work out what it means. Strong analysis moves through four levels:

| Level | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Describe | What happened? | September sales were ₹51.0 lakh, up 8% on August. |
| Diagnose | Why did it happen? | Almost all of the increase came from Kurtis, mainly through Online. |
| Assess | Does it matter? | Online Kurtis are now a meaningful share of sales, and COD is high in that segment. |
| Recommend | What should we do? | Stock up Kurtis for Diwali; trial a small prepaid discount online to reduce COD. |

Your executive summary should reach at least "assess" for every insight, and "recommend" for most.

## Asking better analytical questions

Weak questions get weak insights. Upgrade them with these patterns:

- **Compare, don't just count.** Not "what were sales?" but "how do sales compare with last month, last year and target?"
- **Decompose the change.** "Sales rose ₹4 lakh — which stores, categories and items explain that ₹4 lakh?"
- **Split the average.** Averages hide things. "Average invoice value is flat" may hide one store rising and one falling.
- **Look for concentration.** "What share of sales comes from the top 10 items or top 20 customers?"
- **Check the rate, not just the volume.** For LeadLoop: more leads from IndiaMART is good only if the win rate holds.
- **Ask what's missing.** "Are there stores, months or categories with zero sales that should have some?"

Use AI as a thinking partner to generate questions before you analyse:

```text
I'm analysing Q2 (Jul–Sep 2026) sales for a Jaipur clothing retailer with 2 stores and an online channel,
ahead of Diwali stock planning. Data: invoice lines with Date, Store, Category, Item, Qty, Amount, Payment Mode, Customer City.
Suggest 10 analytical questions that would most help the owner decide what to stock and where to focus.
For each, say which columns and which comparison I'd need.
```

Then choose the 6–8 most decision-relevant ones and answer them with your own pivots, formulas or verified AI analysis.

## What makes an insight worth including

Run each candidate finding through four filters:

1. **Is it true?** Verified with the checks from Lesson 4.2.
2. **Is it material?** A 50% rise from ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 might not matter in a ₹50 lakh business. Express effects in rupees, not just percentages.
3. **Is it new?** "Saree sales rise before festivals" is known to everyone in Jaipur. "Saree sales rose less than last year" is news.
4. **Is it actionable?** Can someone do something differently because of it?

Keep the five that pass best. If you have only three strong insights, write three — padding weakens the whole document.

## The structure of a one-page summary

```text
Title: [Business] — [Period] performance summary
Date | Prepared by | Data source and as-of date

Headline (1–2 sentences): the single most important message.

Key numbers (small table): Sales, MoM %, vs target, Invoices, Avg invoice value.

Five insights, each:
  Bold one-line headline stating the finding
  2–3 sentences of evidence (numbers in ₹ and %)
  "Implication / suggested action:" one sentence

Data notes: period, exclusions, GST treatment, known limitations.
```

Insight headlines should be complete sentences that state the point, not topic labels. Compare:

- Weak: "Category performance"
- Strong: "Kurtis drove nearly all of September's growth, almost entirely online"

A reader who only scans the bold lines should still get the full story.

## Writing with AI — without losing the truth

AI is excellent at turning your verified findings into crisp prose. The key is to give it **your numbers** and forbid it from adding new ones.

```text
Write a one-page executive summary for the owner of Pink City Fabrics, Jaipur, for Q2 FY2026-27 (Jul–Sep 2026).
Use ONLY the verified facts below. Do not add any numbers, causes or claims that are not listed.
If something seems to need more evidence, say "to be confirmed" instead of guessing.
Structure: headline, key numbers table, 5 insights (bold one-line headline, 2–3 sentences of evidence, one suggested action), data notes.
Tone: direct, plain English, no jargon. Amounts in ₹ lakh with one decimal.

Verified facts:
1. [fact with numbers]
2. ...
Data notes: [exclusions, GST-inclusive, etc.]
```

After the draft comes back:

- **Check every number** against your fact list. Any number not in your list must go.
- **Check causal language.** "Because of the influencer campaign" is a claim; if you didn't verify it, change it to "coinciding with" or remove it.
- **Cut 20%.** First drafts are always too long. Executives reward brevity.
- **Read it aloud.** If a sentence is awkward to say, it's awkward to read.

## A worked example (illustrative)

Here is how one insight might look, using made-up figures from the practice dataset:

```text
2. Online COD is rising and now accounts for over half of online orders.
COD share of online invoices rose from 41% in July to 54% in September, while online sales grew 18%.
Return-to-origin data isn't in this dataset, so the cost impact is to be confirmed.
Suggested action: pull RTO data from the courier dashboard; if COD returns are high, test a small prepaid incentive in October.
```

Notice: specific numbers, honesty about what the data can't show, and a proportionate next step rather than a sweeping recommendation.

## Presenting it

- Send the summary as the email body or a one-page PDF, with the dashboard or MIS workbook attached for anyone who wants detail.
- Lead the email with the headline sentence — many readers will stop there.
- In a meeting, talk through the five bold lines and let the evidence support questions.
- End with the decisions you need: "Approve additional Kurti stock of ₹X lakh by 25 October?"

## Try it yourself

This capstone takes 45–60 minutes.

1. Use your clean Pink City Fabrics data (or LeadLoop's leads, or a masked work dataset).
2. Ask an AI for 10 analytical questions, choose 6–8, and answer them with pivots or verified analysis.
3. Filter findings through true / material / new / actionable and keep the best five.
4. Write your verified facts list, then use the prompt above to get a draft.
5. Edit the draft: check every number, fix causal claims, cut 20%.
6. Save the final one-pager alongside your dashboard and MIS report — together, they form your portfolio from this course.

## Key takeaways

- Move every insight from describe to diagnose to assess, and recommend where you can.
- Ask comparative, decomposing questions; let AI help brainstorm them.
- Keep only insights that are true, material, new and actionable — five or fewer.
- Write bold, complete-sentence headlines so a scanner gets the whole story.
- Give AI only verified facts, forbid new numbers, and edit hard before sending.
