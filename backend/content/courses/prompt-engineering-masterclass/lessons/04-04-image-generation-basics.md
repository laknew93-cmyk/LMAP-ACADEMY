Many chat assistants can now generate images, and dedicated image tools are widely used for marketing, presentations and social media. Image prompts work differently from text prompts. This lesson gives you a reliable structure and the main pitfalls to avoid.

## How image prompts differ

Text prompts are instructions. Image prompts are closer to **descriptions of a picture that should exist**. The model needs to know what's in the scene, how it looks, and how it's framed. Vague prompts give stock-photo-style results; specific visual detail gives distinctive ones.

## The image prompt structure

Cover these elements, roughly in this order:

1. **Subject** — who or what is the focus.
2. **Action or pose** — what is happening.
3. **Setting** — where, and relevant background details.
4. **Style** — photo, watercolour, flat vector illustration, 3D render, line art.
5. **Composition** — close-up, wide shot, top-down, rule of thirds, space for text.
6. **Lighting and colour** — soft morning light, warm tones, brand colours.
7. **Mood** — calm, festive, professional, playful.
8. **Format** — aspect ratio (square for Instagram feed, vertical for stories, wide for slides), where the tool supports it.

### Before and after

Before:

```text
Picture of a bakery for Instagram.
```

After:

```text
A warm, realistic photo of a small neighbourhood bakery counter in Pune
in the early morning. A glass display holds nankhatai, rusk and a
jaggery millet cake on a wooden stand. Soft golden sunlight through the
window, steam rising from a cup of cutting chai on the counter.
Shallow depth of field, focus on the cake. Earthy browns and cream tones.
Calm, homely mood. Square format, with empty space at the top for text.
```

The second prompt gives a usable, on-brand image far more often.

## Style vocabulary that works

| Want | Useful words |
|---|---|
| Realistic photo | "photograph", "natural light", "shallow depth of field", "35mm" |
| Clean marketing graphic | "flat vector illustration", "minimal", "solid background" |
| Friendly explainer | "simple cartoon illustration", "rounded shapes", "pastel palette" |
| Premium product | "studio product shot", "soft shadows", "white seamless background" |
| Traditional Indian art feel | "inspired by Madhubani folk art patterns", "Warli-style line figures" |

Describe styles and movements rather than naming living artists. Copying a specific living artist's style raises ethical and sometimes legal questions, and many tools restrict it.

## Text in images

Image models have improved at rendering text, but it's still a common failure point, especially for long phrases or non-Latin scripts. Practical approach:

- Keep in-image text very short (1-4 words), and put it in quotes: `a sign reading "OPEN"`.
- For posters, flyers and social posts, generate the image with **empty space** and add text yourself in Canva, PowerPoint or a similar tool. You get correct spelling, your brand fonts, and easy edits.

## Iterating on images

Image work is highly iterative. Change one thing at a time:

```text
Same image, but make it evening with warm string lights, and move the cake
to the left third of the frame.
```

If a result is close, many tools let you edit a selected region or ask for variations. Save prompts that produce good results along with the image so you can recreate the look later.

## Using a text assistant to write image prompts

A neat trick: ask a chat assistant to write the image prompt for you.

```text
I need a hero image for a LinkedIn post announcing our free GST-filing
webinar for small shop owners in Tier-2 cities.
Write 3 detailed image-generation prompts in different styles (photo,
flat illustration, minimal 3D). Each should include subject, setting,
composition, lighting, colours (our brand: teal and white) and leave
space at the top for a headline. No text inside the image.
```

## Responsible image use

- **Real people:** Don't generate realistic images of real, identifiable people without consent, and never in misleading or harmful situations. Many tools block this anyway.
- **Disclosure:** For news-like or documentary contexts, don't present AI images as real photographs. Follow your platform's and employer's labelling rules.
- **Products:** Don't use AI images that misrepresent what a customer will actually receive. A Jaipur jewellery seller should photograph the actual necklace for the listing, and use AI for lifestyle backgrounds or concept visuals.
- **Rights:** Check your tool's terms on commercial use, and your company's policy, before using images in paid ads or client work.
- **Bias:** Image models can default to stereotypes. Specify diversity deliberately where relevant: age, gender, skin tone, region, clothing.

## Try it yourself

Spend 15 minutes, using any image tool you have access to.

1. Choose a real need: a social post, a slide visual, a blog header, a classroom illustration.
2. Write a one-line prompt and generate an image.
3. Rewrite it with all eight elements (subject, action, setting, style, composition, lighting, mood, format) and generate again.
4. Make two single-change iterations.
5. Ask a chat assistant to write three alternative prompts for the same need, and try the best one.
6. Save your best prompt and a note on what worked in your library.

## Key takeaways

- Image prompts describe a picture: subject, action, setting, style, composition, lighting, mood, format.
- Specific visual details beat generic adjectives like "beautiful" or "professional".
- Keep in-image text minimal; add headlines yourself in a design tool.
- Iterate one change at a time and save prompts that work.
- Use images responsibly: no misleading depictions of real people or products, and check usage rights.
