Writing is where most people first use AI, and where generic output is most obvious. This lesson gives you prompts that produce writing in *your* voice, for a specific reader, with a clear purpose.

## The three things every writing prompt needs

1. **Reader** — who exactly will read this, and what do they already know?
2. **Purpose** — what should the reader think, feel or do afterwards?
3. **Voice** — how should it sound? Ideally shown with a sample.

Most bland AI writing is missing at least two of these.

## Emails

Before:

```text
Write an email asking my manager for leave.
```

After:

```text
Write a short email to my manager, Suresh, requesting leave.

Facts:
- Dates: 12-16 Nov (5 working days), for my sister's wedding in Madurai
- My open work: monthly vendor reconciliation, due 10 Nov — I'll finish it early
- Backup: Divya has agreed to handle urgent vendor queries

Purpose: get approval without back-and-forth.
Tone: polite, confident, not over-apologetic. Our office uses first names.
Length: under 100 words. Include a subject line.
```

The facts block makes the email complete; the purpose line makes it decisive.

## Matching your own voice

Paste 2-3 samples of your own writing and ask the model to analyse them first:

```text
Here are three LinkedIn posts I wrote that performed well.
First, describe my writing style in 5 bullet points (sentence length,
vocabulary, how I open, how I close, use of humour or emojis).
Then write a new post in exactly that style about [TOPIC].

<my_posts>
[POST 1]
---
[POST 2]
---
[POST 3]
</my_posts>
```

The style description is useful on its own. Save it and add it to future writing prompts as "My style guide".

## Rewriting and editing

Editing prompts work best when you say what *kind* of edit you want:

| Edit type | Prompt phrase |
|---|---|
| Light proofread | "Fix grammar and spelling only. Do not change wording or tone." |
| Clarity edit | "Simplify sentences for a Class 8 reading level. Keep all facts." |
| Shorten | "Cut to 150 words. Keep the 3 main points and the call to action." |
| Tone shift | "Make this firmer but still polite; it's our second payment reminder." |
| Localise | "Adapt for small business owners in Tier-2 cities; use ₹ and familiar examples." |

Ask to see changes when it matters:

```text
Edit the paragraph below for clarity. Then list each change you made and why,
so I can accept or reject them.
```

## Long-form content

For blogs, reports and articles, don't ask for the full piece in one go. Use a mini-chain:

1. **Angle** — "Suggest 5 angles for a blog on GST for freelance designers. For each, give the reader's main question."
2. **Outline** — "Create a detailed outline for angle 3 with H2s, key points under each, and where an example would help."
3. **Draft by section** — "Write section 2 only, 250 words, using this real example: ..."
4. **Polish** — "Write an intro hook and a conclusion with a clear next step."

You stay in control of the argument; the AI does the heavy drafting.

## Writing for different formats

The same content needs different shapes:

```text
Turn the blog post below into:
1. A LinkedIn post (under 200 words, hook in the first line, no hashtag spam)
2. An Instagram carousel outline (7 slides, max 15 words per slide)
3. A WhatsApp broadcast message for existing clients (under 60 words, one link)
4. Three tweet-length takeaways

<blog>
...
</blog>
```

This "repurposing" prompt is one of the highest-value prompts for marketers and small business owners.

## Teachers and trainers

```text
You are an experienced Class 7 science teacher in a CBSE school.
Create a 40-minute lesson plan on "Acids, Bases and Salts".
Include: learning objectives, a 5-minute hook using household items
available in an Indian kitchen, one group activity, 5 exit-ticket questions
(2 easy, 2 medium, 1 challenging), and a 2-line note for students who
were absent. Format with clear headings and timings.
```

## Avoiding the "AI voice"

Readers increasingly spot AI-sounding text: over-enthusiastic openers, words like "delve", "tapestry", "in today's fast-paced world", tidy groups of three, and empty conclusions. Add a banned list to your style guide:

```text
Avoid: "In today's fast-paced world", "delve", "unlock", "game-changer",
"elevate", "seamless", rhetorical questions as openers, and ending with a
generic summary. Prefer concrete details and plain verbs.
```

And always do a final read yourself. Your name is on it.

## Try it yourself

Spend 15-20 minutes.

1. Collect 2-3 pieces of your own writing (posts, emails, a report section).
2. Use the voice-analysis prompt to generate your 5-bullet style guide, then add a banned-words list.
3. Write one real email using the Reader / Purpose / Facts / Tone structure.
4. Take any long piece (yours or a public article) and run the repurposing prompt for 3 formats.
5. Save your style guide and the two best prompts to your library.

## Key takeaways

- Every writing prompt needs a reader, a purpose and a voice.
- Put facts in a separate block so drafts are complete and accurate.
- Build a personal style guide from your own samples and reuse it.
- Write long-form content as a chain: angle, outline, section drafts, polish.
- Ban AI clichés explicitly, and always do the final edit yourself.
