A good resume gets you noticed; a strong LinkedIn profile and confident interviews get you hired. In this lesson you'll use AI to improve your LinkedIn presence, write networking messages, and practise interviews in the formats Indian employers actually use.

## Part 1: LinkedIn with AI

### Your headline

Your headline appears everywhere — search results, comments, connection requests. "Looking for opportunities" wastes it. Ask:

```text
Write 5 LinkedIn headline options (under 220 characters) for me. I am a B.Com graduate with 3 years of experience in accounts payable and GST filing at a manufacturing company in Ludhiana, now looking for finance operations roles. Include relevant keywords recruiters search for. No emojis, no buzzwords.
```

Pick one and adjust it to sound like you.

### Your About section

```text
Write a LinkedIn About section (150-200 words) in first person based on my resume below. Structure: what I do, 2-3 specific achievements, what I'm looking for next. Warm, confident, not boastful. Do not add anything not in my resume.
[paste resume without contact details]
```

### Posts that build visibility

You don't need to become an influencer, but occasional thoughtful posts help recruiters find you. AI can help you turn your experiences into posts:

```text
I just completed a course on AI tools for office work. Help me write a short LinkedIn post (under 150 words) sharing 3 practical things I learned and how I'll use them in finance operations. Genuine tone, not salesy, end with a question to invite comments.
```

Always edit AI drafts so they reflect your real experience and voice. Readers quickly notice posts that sound generic.

### Networking messages

Cold messages to strangers work best when short and specific:

```text
Write a LinkedIn connection note (under 300 characters) to a Finance Manager at a company I'm interested in. Mention that I read her post about automating vendor reconciliation, and ask if she'd be open to a 10-minute chat about her team's work. Polite, no direct request for a job.
```

Also useful: follow-up messages to recruiters after applying, and thank-you notes after interviews.

## Part 2: Mock interviews with AI

AI assistants make excellent practice partners: available any time, patient, and never judgemental. Many apps also support voice, so you can practise speaking your answers aloud — closer to the real thing.

### Setting up a mock interview

```text
Act as an interviewer for the role of Customer Success Associate at a SaaS company in Bengaluru. I am a career returner with 5 years of prior experience in banking customer service.
Interview me one question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next question.
After each answer, give brief feedback: what was good, what to improve, and a stronger sample answer.
Start with "Tell me about yourself".
```

"One question at a time" is important — otherwise the assistant may list all questions at once.

### Indian interview formats to practise

Indian hiring processes have their own patterns. Practise the ones relevant to you:

| Round | What to practise with AI |
|---|---|
| HR screening call | "Tell me about yourself", current and expected CTC, notice period, reason for job change, relocation |
| Technical / functional round | Role-specific questions — ask AI to quiz you on your domain |
| Managerial round | Situational questions: conflicts, deadlines, mistakes, priorities |
| Group discussion | Common in campus and some bulk hiring — practise structuring points on a topic |
| Case or assignment | Ask AI for a sample case in your field and walk through your approach |
| Final HR / fitment | Career goals, why this company, questions you'd ask them |

### Tricky questions Indian candidates face

Practise honest, confident answers to:

- "Why is there a gap in your resume?" (career returners)
- "You spent three years preparing for government exams — why move to the private sector now?"
- "Why do you want to leave your current company?"
- "What is your expected CTC?"
- "Are you comfortable with rotational shifts or travel?"

```text
I took a 3-year break for my father's medical care. Help me prepare a 45-second answer to "Why is there a gap in your resume?" that is honest, brief, and moves the conversation towards my readiness to work now. Then ask me the question and give feedback on my version.
```

For salary questions, use AI to help you plan *how* to discuss it, but research realistic salary ranges yourself using job portals, salary-survey sites and people in the industry — AI figures for salaries may be outdated or unreliable.

### Practise with the STAR method

For behavioural questions ("Tell me about a time when…"), the STAR structure helps: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

```text
Here is my rough answer to "Tell me about a time you handled a difficult customer": [your answer]. Restructure it using STAR, keep it under 90 seconds when spoken, and keep all facts exactly as I gave them.
```

## A note on honesty

Using AI to *prepare* for interviews is smart. Using AI to secretly answer questions during a live interview or online assessment is dishonest, is often against the employer's rules, and tends to be noticed. Your goal is to become genuinely confident — which is exactly what practice gives you.

## Try it yourself

Allow about 20 minutes.

1. Rewrite your LinkedIn headline and About section with the prompts above. Update your profile if you're happy with them.
2. Write one networking message to a real person in a company or role you're interested in. Edit it before sending.
3. Run a mock interview for a real role you want: at least five questions, one at a time, with feedback.
4. Prepare a STAR answer for one behavioural question and one answer to a tricky question from the list.
5. If your app supports voice, repeat two answers out loud.
6. Save your mock-interview prompt as Prompt #13 in your library.

## Key takeaways

- AI can sharpen your LinkedIn headline, About section, posts and networking messages — always edit for your real voice.
- Set up mock interviews with "one question at a time" and ask for feedback after each answer.
- Practise the rounds Indian employers use: HR screening, functional, managerial, GD and fitment.
- Prepare honest answers for career gaps, job changes and salary questions; verify salary ranges yourself.
- Use AI to prepare, never to cheat during live interviews or tests.
