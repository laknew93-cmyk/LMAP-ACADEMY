Reports, proposals, policies and SOPs take hours because of the blank page and the endless restructuring. In this lesson you'll learn a step-by-step method to produce clear documents with AI while keeping your own thinking at the centre.

## The outline-first method

The most common mistake is asking an AI to "write a report on X" in one go. You get something long, generic and hard to fix. Instead, work in stages:

1. **Brief** — tell the assistant the purpose, reader and key facts.
2. **Outline** — ask for a structure only. Fix it.
3. **Draft section by section** — write one section at a time, feeding in your data.
4. **Edit** — tighten, check facts, adjust tone.
5. **Polish** — summary, headings, formatting.

This keeps you in control of the argument while the AI does the heavy lifting on wording.

## Step 1 and 2: Brief and outline

```text
I need to write a 2-page internal report for our regional manager.
Topic: why customer complaints at our Nagpur branch rose in the last quarter, and what we propose to do.
Reader: regional manager — busy, wants facts and clear recommendations.
Key facts:
- Complaints rose from 38 to 61 between Q1 and Q2
- Main reasons: delayed home deliveries (27), billing errors (18), staff behaviour (9), others (7)
- Two delivery staff left in May; replacements joined in July
- Billing software update in June caused errors for 3 weeks
Before writing, give me an outline with section headings and 1-line notes on what each covers.
```

Review the outline. Move sections, remove what doesn't matter, add what's missing. Only then move on.

## Step 3: Draft section by section

```text
Good. Now write only the "Root causes" section, about 200 words, using the facts I gave. Use a short table for the complaint categories. Plain business English.
```

Drafting one section at a time gives better quality and makes it easier to spot errors. If the AI invents a number you didn't give it, delete it immediately — never let made-up figures into a report.

## Step 4: Editing with AI

Once you have a full draft (whether written by you or the AI), the assistant is a superb editor:

```text
Edit this report for clarity and brevity. Cut at least 20% of the words without losing any facts. Fix grammar. Keep Indian English spelling. Show me the edited version only.
```

Other useful editing prompts:

- `Rewrite the opening paragraph so the main finding is in the first sentence.`
- `Point out any claims in this report that are not supported by data.`
- `Which sections would a busy manager skip? How can I make them sharper?`
- `Convert this paragraph into 4 bullet points.`

## Step 5: The executive summary

Busy readers often only read the top. Ask for a summary last, once the content is final:

```text
Write a 5-line executive summary of this report: the problem, the main cause, the 3 recommendations, and what decision I need from the reader.
```

## Templates for common documents

### Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

```text
Write an SOP for how our front-desk staff should handle walk-in customer complaints at our salon in Kochi.
Include: purpose, who it applies to, step-by-step process (numbered), what to record, when to escalate to the manager, and a short do's and don'ts list.
Language: simple English that new staff can follow.
```

### Business proposal

```text
Draft a one-page proposal from our company, BrightNest Interiors (Ahmedabad), to a corporate client for redesigning their 3,000 sq ft office reception and meeting rooms.
Sections: understanding of the need, our approach, timeline (6 weeks), what's included, next steps.
Do not include prices — I will add them. Professional and confident, not salesy.
```

### Policy document

```text
Draft a simple work-from-home policy for a 25-person accounting firm. Cover eligibility, days allowed, availability hours, data security, and approval process. Use clear headings and short paragraphs. Mark any points where I need to make a company decision with [DECIDE].
```

The `[DECIDE]` trick is useful: it stops the AI from silently inventing rules your company never agreed to.

## Rewriting and simplifying existing documents

Often you're not writing from scratch but improving something old:

- **Simplify**: "Rewrite this policy so a new employee can understand it in one read."
- **Restructure**: "Reorganise this document into: background, problem, options, recommendation."
- **Change audience**: "Rewrite this technical note for a non-technical client."
- **Shorten**: "Turn this 4-page document into a 1-page brief."

## Formatting tips

- Ask for headings and bullet points — they paste cleanly into Word or Google Docs.
- Ask for tables when comparing options; most assistants format them well.
- If you plan to paste into Word, ask for "plain text with headings, no markdown symbols" to avoid stray asterisks.

## Your role stays central

AI can produce a well-organised document quickly, but the judgement is yours: which facts matter, what you recommend, and what your organisation can actually commit to. Read every sentence before your name goes on it.

## Try it yourself

Take 20 minutes to produce a real one-page document.

1. Pick a document you need: a status report, a proposal, a simple SOP, or a summary of a project you've done.
2. Write a brief using the Step 1 example: purpose, reader, key facts (at least four).
3. Ask for an outline only. Make at least one change to it.
4. Draft two sections, one at a time.
5. Run the "cut 20%" editing prompt on the result.
6. Ask for a 5-line executive summary.
7. Check every number and claim against your own facts, then save your brief prompt as Prompt #4 in your library.

## Key takeaways

- Don't ask for a whole report in one go — brief, outline, draft by section, edit, then summarise.
- Feed in your real facts; delete any figures the AI invents.
- AI is an excellent editor: ask it to cut words, sharpen openings and flag unsupported claims.
- Use markers like [DECIDE] so the AI doesn't invent policies or commitments.
- The judgement and final responsibility for the document remain yours.
