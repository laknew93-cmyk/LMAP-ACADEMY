Email and messaging probably eat more of your working day than you realise. In this lesson you'll learn to draft, reply to and polish emails and WhatsApp-style messages with AI — and, most importantly, to control the tone.

## Where AI saves the most time in communication

Most people don't struggle with *what* to say. They struggle with *how* to say it: too blunt, too long, too apologetic, too casual. AI assistants are excellent at that "how". Typical wins:

- Turning three rough bullet points into a clear email
- Replying to a long, confusing email thread
- Saying "no" politely to a client or senior
- Following up without sounding pushy
- Writing to someone senior, or in a formal setting, when you're nervous
- Shortening a rambling message to something people will actually read

## Drafting from bullet points

The fastest method: write what you want to say in rough notes, then let AI shape it.

```text
Turn these notes into a clear, polite email to our vendor, Shree Ganesh Packaging.
Notes:
- last 2 deliveries late by 4-5 days
- affecting our Diwali dispatch to retailers
- need confirmation that the 15 Oct order will arrive on time
- if not, we may need to look at another supplier for this season
Tone: firm but respectful — we want to continue working with them.
Length: under 150 words. Add a clear subject line.
```

Your notes carry the facts; the AI handles the structure and politeness. You stay in control of the content.

## Replying to long threads

When an email thread has 12 replies and you've been added at the end, paste it in and ask:

```text
Below is an email thread. First, summarise in 4 bullet points what has been discussed and what is being asked of me. Then draft a short reply that answers the open question about the revised delivery date, which is 22 October.
[paste thread]
```

Before pasting, remove anything confidential your company doesn't allow in AI tools — we'll cover the rules in Module 5.

## Mastering tone control

Tone is where AI really shines. You can take the same message and dial it up or down.

### The tone dial

Try asking for the same message at different points on the scale:

| Tone | When to use it |
|---|---|
| Warm and friendly | Colleagues you know, long-term clients |
| Professional and neutral | Most business email |
| Formal | Government offices, banks, senior leadership, legal matters |
| Firm | Overdue payments, repeated delays, boundary-setting |
| Apologetic | When you or your company made a mistake |
| Concise | Busy readers, mobile messages |

A useful prompt:

```text
Here is my draft message. Rewrite it in three versions: (1) warm and friendly, (2) professional and neutral, (3) firm. Keep each under 80 words.
[paste draft]
```

### Saying no without burning bridges

Neha, a freelance graphic designer in Jaipur, often gets asked for "small extra changes" outside the agreed scope. She uses:

```text
A client has asked for 3 additional poster designs that were not part of our agreed scope. I want to say these will cost ₹1,500 per design extra, without sounding rude or greedy. We have a good relationship and I want to keep it. Write a short, friendly WhatsApp message.
```

### Payment follow-ups

Chasing payments is awkward for many small business owners. AI helps you write a sequence that gets firmer step by step:

```text
Write 3 payment reminder messages for an unpaid invoice of ₹42,000 (Invoice #INV-218, due 30 September) to a client company.
Message 1: gentle reminder, 3 days after due date.
Message 2: polite but clearer, 10 days after.
Message 3: firm, 20 days after, mentioning we may pause further work.
Each under 70 words. Mention that payment can be made by bank transfer or UPI.
```

## Messages beyond email

The same techniques work for:

- **WhatsApp business messages** — order confirmations, festive greetings, delivery updates
- **Teams or Slack messages** — status updates, quick requests to colleagues
- **LinkedIn messages** — networking notes to people you don't know (more in Module 5)
- **Notices** — society notices, office circulars, school communication

Just tell the assistant the platform: "This is a WhatsApp message, so keep it short with no subject line."

## Keep your own voice

AI-written messages can sound generic or overly polished, and people notice. Some ways to keep them sounding like you:

- Paste two or three emails you've written before and say "Match my writing style."
- Ask it to avoid stock phrases: "Do not use 'I hope this email finds you well' or 'Please do the needful'."
- Always read the final version out loud mentally. If it doesn't sound like you, edit it.
- Add one personal line yourself — a reference to your last conversation, for example.

## Review before you send

Every AI-drafted message needs a 30-second check:

1. Are all names, dates, amounts and invoice numbers correct?
2. Did it add any promise you didn't intend ("We will refund in full")?
3. Is the tone right for this specific person?
4. Is it short enough for the reader?

## Try it yourself

Set aside 15 minutes and use real messages from your own week.

1. Find one email or message you need to send (or recently sent) — a request, follow-up, complaint or update.
2. Write your rough notes in 3-5 bullet points.
3. Use the "Turn these notes into…" prompt above, specifying audience, tone and length.
4. Ask for the same message in three tones: warm, neutral and firm. Pick the one that fits best.
5. Run the four-point review checklist and fix anything wrong.
6. Save the prompt that worked best as Prompt #2 in your prompt library.

## Key takeaways

- Write the facts yourself in rough notes; let AI handle structure, clarity and politeness.
- Paste long threads and ask for a summary of what's being asked before drafting a reply.
- Specify tone, platform and word limit — and ask for multiple tone versions when unsure.
- Remove stock phrases and add a personal line so messages still sound like you.
- Always check names, numbers and promises before you hit send.
