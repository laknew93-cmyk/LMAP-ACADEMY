There are several big AI assistants, and people often ask "Which one is the best?" The honest answer is: it depends on the task and the tools you already use. This lesson gives you a practical way to choose.

## The main assistants at a glance

Four assistants cover most everyday work. All of them can write, summarise, explain and brainstorm. The differences are in their strengths, where they live, and what they connect to.

| Assistant | Made by | Where it fits best |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | OpenAI | All-rounder; huge range of features; very popular, so lots of tutorials and shared prompts |
| Claude | Anthropic | Long documents, careful reasoning, natural-sounding writing |
| Gemini | Google | Works closely with Google Search and Google Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive) |
| Microsoft Copilot | Microsoft | Works inside Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams) and Windows |

All four have free versions you can use with a personal account, and paid plans with more usage and features. Plans and limits change often, so check each product's own website for current details rather than relying on any list (including this one).

## What each one tends to do well

### ChatGPT

ChatGPT is the assistant most people try first. It's a strong all-rounder for drafting, explaining, brainstorming and analysing files. It usually offers features like file uploads, image understanding, web search, voice conversations and saved custom instructions. Because it's so widely used, you'll find countless example prompts shared online.

**Good first choice for:** general daily use, brainstorming, quick drafts, learning new topics.

### Claude

Claude is known for handling long documents well and for writing that sounds natural rather than robotic. Many professionals like it for reading long reports, contracts or policy documents, and for careful, structured thinking where they want the assistant to point out problems rather than just agree.

**Good first choice for:** summarising long PDFs, rewriting documents, nuanced writing, reviewing your work critically.

### Gemini

Gemini's big advantage is its connection to Google. It can draw on Google Search for current information, and in Google Workspace accounts it can work inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets and Drive (depending on your plan and your organisation's settings). If your life already runs on Gmail and Google Drive, Gemini fits in naturally.

**Good first choice for:** research needing current information, Gmail and Google Docs users, Android phone users.

### Microsoft Copilot

Copilot is Microsoft's assistant. The free version works like a general chat assistant. In organisations with the right Microsoft 365 licence, Copilot can work inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams — summarising email threads, drafting documents from your files, and recapping meetings.

**Good first choice for:** people whose office runs on Microsoft 365, especially Outlook and Teams users.

We'll go deeper into Copilot and Gemini inside office apps in Module 4.

## How to choose: four practical questions

Rather than looking for "the best", ask yourself:

1. **Where does my work already live?** If your company uses Outlook and Teams, learn Copilot. If you live in Gmail and Google Docs, learn Gemini. Assistants built into your tools save copy-paste effort.
2. **What does my company allow?** Many companies approve one specific tool for work data. Use that one for anything confidential. (More on this in Module 5.)
3. **What is the task?** For long documents, try Claude or a tool with a large document capacity. For current facts, use an assistant that searches the web and shows sources.
4. **Which one do I enjoy using?** Seriously — the tool you open every day beats the "best" tool you rarely use.

## The "second opinion" habit

A smart habit used by many professionals: for important work, run the same prompt in two different assistants. For example, Arjun, a sales manager in Chennai, drafts his quarterly proposal in ChatGPT and then pastes it into Claude with "Point out anything unclear or unconvincing in this proposal." Different assistants catch different issues.

This is especially useful for:

- Important emails to senior leaders or clients
- Fact-heavy research (if two assistants disagree, check the source)
- Interview answers and cover letters

## A sample comparison you can run

Use the same prompt in two assistants and compare:

```text
I run a small home-baking business in Pune called "Sugar & Saffron". Write 3 short Instagram captions for our new eggless chocolate cake, each under 30 words, friendly tone, with 3 relevant hashtags each.
```

Notice differences in tone, creativity, length and how closely each followed your instructions. There is no single right winner — you are learning each tool's "personality".

## Free versus paid: a sensible approach

You can complete this entire course using free versions. A sensible path:

- Start with free versions of two assistants for a month.
- Notice which one you open most and where you hit limits.
- Only then consider a paid plan for that one tool, if the time saved justifies the cost for you.
- If your employer provides an approved AI tool, use that for work and keep personal accounts for personal tasks.

## Try it yourself

Spend about 20 minutes on this side-by-side test.

1. Open two different assistants in two browser tabs (for example ChatGPT and Gemini, or Claude and Copilot).
2. Paste the same three prompts into each:
   - `Write a polite email to my landlord asking to fix a leaking bathroom tap this week.`
   - `Explain what a mutual fund SIP is in 5 simple bullet points for a college student.`
   - `What are the three most important things to check before signing a rental agreement in India?`
3. For each prompt, note which answer was clearer, which followed instructions better, and which you would actually use.
4. For the third prompt, check whether either assistant showed sources.
5. Write down your "default assistant" for now and one backup.

## Key takeaways

- ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot can all handle everyday writing and research tasks.
- Choose based on where your work lives, what your company allows, and the task at hand.
- Gemini fits Google users; Copilot fits Microsoft 365 users; Claude is strong with long documents; ChatGPT is a versatile all-rounder.
- For important work, get a second opinion from a different assistant.
- Free versions are enough to learn; decide on paid plans only after real use.
