Many startups, schools, small businesses and freelancers across India run on Gmail, Google Docs, Sheets and Drive. Gemini brings AI into these tools. This lesson covers what Gemini can typically do inside Google Workspace and how to use it well.

## Gemini app versus Gemini in Workspace

As with Copilot, there are two ways to meet Gemini:

- **The Gemini app** — a chat assistant on the web and on phones (on many Android phones it can act as the built-in assistant). It works like other chat assistants and can draw on Google Search.
- **Gemini in Google Workspace** — AI features inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Drive and Meet, usually shown as a Gemini icon or side panel.

Which features you see depends on your account type (personal or work), your plan, and what your organisation's admin has enabled. Google changes features and plan names regularly, so treat this lesson as a guide to capabilities rather than an exact map of buttons.

A useful feature to know about: in many setups, the Gemini app can connect to your Gmail, Drive and other Google apps (with your permission), so you can ask questions like "Find the email from the CA about the GST filing date."

## Gemini in Gmail

**Common uses:**

- Summarise a long email thread
- Draft a new email from a short instruction ("Help me write")
- Refine a draft: make it more formal, shorter or more detailed
- Suggest replies based on the conversation
- Find information across your inbox ("What did the courier company say about the refund?")

**Example prompt:**

```text
Write an email to the parents of my Class 6 tuition batch informing them that classes on 2 and 3 November are cancelled for Diwali, and that a make-up class will be held on Sunday, 9 November, 10 am to 12 pm. Warm and clear.
```

## Gemini in Google Docs

**Common uses:**

- Draft a document from a prompt
- Rewrite selected text — shorten, elaborate, change tone, convert to bullets
- Summarise the document
- In many setups, reference other files from your Drive while drafting

**Example prompt in the side panel:**

```text
Using the notes in this document, create a one-page proposal for a corporate Diwali gifting order of 200 hampers. Include: options, timeline, what we need from the client, and next steps. Leave prices as [PRICE].
```

## Gemini in Google Sheets

**Common uses:**

- Create tables and templates from a description ("Create an expense tracker for a small café")
- Suggest formulas
- Summarise and analyse data, and suggest charts
- Help organise and categorise entries

**Example prompt:**

```text
Create a tracker for our Instagram orders with columns: order date, customer name, product, quantity, amount, payment mode (UPI/cash/card), delivery status. Add sample rows I can delete later.
```

As always, verify formulas and totals before relying on them.

## Gemini in Drive

In many setups, Gemini in Drive can summarise files and folders and answer questions across your documents. For example: "Summarise the three vendor quotations in this folder and compare their delivery timelines." This saves you from opening each file — but open the original before making a decision.

## Gemini in Google Meet

Depending on your plan, Gemini can help with meetings in Google Meet — for example taking notes automatically and creating a summary document after the call, often shared with the organiser. Settings and availability vary, so check what your account offers. We cover meeting workflows in detail in the next lesson.

## Gemini app: research with Google Search

Outside Workspace, the Gemini app is useful for research because it can draw on Google Search. Some versions offer a deep research style mode that reads many web pages and builds a report. The verification habits from Lesson 3.1 still apply: open the sources, check the numbers.

## Personal account versus work account

This distinction matters a lot for privacy:

| | Personal Google account | Work/school Workspace account |
|---|---|---|
| Who controls settings | You | Your organisation's admin |
| Data use | Governed by Google's consumer terms and your activity settings | Governed by your organisation's agreement with Google |
| Best for | Personal tasks, learning, side projects | Company work, as approved by your employer |

Don't move work documents into a personal account to use AI features your company hasn't approved. Review your activity and privacy settings in your personal Gemini account so you understand what is saved.

## Choosing between Gemini and Copilot

You don't have to pick a side. The practical rule: **use the AI built into the suite your work already uses.** If your company uses Google Workspace, Gemini is the natural fit. If it uses Microsoft 365, Copilot is. For personal tasks, use whichever you prefer.

Deepa, an operations executive at a Gurugram startup that uses Google Workspace, relies on Gemini in Gmail to summarise vendor threads each morning and on Gemini in Sheets to set up trackers. At home, she uses a different assistant for her personal planning. Both are fine — the skill is the same.

## Try it yourself

Spend about 15 minutes exploring.

1. Open Gmail (personal or work) and look for the Gemini icon or "Help me write". Note which features you have.
2. In Gmail, draft a real email using a one-line instruction, then refine it with "shorter" or "more formal".
3. In Google Docs, open or create a document and ask Gemini to summarise it or to draft a one-page plan from your notes.
4. In Google Sheets, ask Gemini to create a simple tracker for something in your life — expenses, job applications or orders.
5. If you don't have Workspace features, use the Gemini app with the same prompts and copy the results across.
6. Save your best Gemini prompt as Prompt #9 in your library.

## Key takeaways

- Gemini exists as a standalone app and as features inside Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive and Meet; availability varies by account and plan.
- Gmail and Docs features help you draft, refine and summarise; Sheets features help build trackers and analyse data.
- The Gemini app's connection to Google Search makes it handy for research — still verify sources.
- Keep work and personal accounts separate and follow your employer's rules.
- Use the AI built into whichever suite your work already runs on.
