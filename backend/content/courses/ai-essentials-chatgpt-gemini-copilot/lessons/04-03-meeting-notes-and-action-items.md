Meetings often end with everyone nodding — and nobody sure who is doing what by when. AI can turn messy discussions into clear notes, decisions and action items in minutes. This lesson shows you how, with or without built-in meeting AI.

## Three ways to capture a meeting

Depending on your tools and your company's rules, you can:

1. **Use built-in meeting AI** — tools like Copilot in Teams, Gemini in Google Meet, or Zoom's AI features can transcribe and summarise meetings if your plan and organisation allow it.
2. **Use a transcript** — many meeting tools can generate a transcript you can paste into an approved assistant.
3. **Use your own rough notes** — type quick notes during the meeting and let AI structure them afterwards.

Option 3 works everywhere, needs no special licence, and is often the most privacy-friendly. Don't underestimate it.

## Consent and etiquette

Before recording or transcribing any meeting:

- Inform participants and, where needed, get their agreement. Most tools show a notice, but say it out loud too.
- Follow your company's policy — some meetings (HR, legal, client negotiations) should not be recorded or fed into AI tools.
- For client calls, check the client is comfortable with recording.

It is both courteous and, in many situations, a legal and contractual matter.

## Turning rough notes into clean minutes

Here's what real notes often look like:

```text
mtg w/ Sharma ji + Anita + me, 14 Oct
- website relaunch - delayed, dev says 2 more wks
- Anita: product photos not done, photographer avail 20th
- budget ok up to 1.5L extra?? Sharma ji to confirm
- launch maybe 10 Nov, not final
- need hindi version of homepage - who?
- me: send revised timeline by Fri
```

Now paste them with this prompt:

```text
Convert these rough meeting notes into clean minutes with these sections:
1. Meeting details (date, attendees)
2. Summary (3 lines)
3. Decisions made
4. Action items — table with: task, owner, due date
5. Open questions / items needing confirmation
Rules: do not invent anything. If an owner or date is unclear, write "TBD". Mark tentative items as tentative.
```

The rules at the end matter. Without them, AI may confidently assign the Hindi homepage task to someone, or turn "launch maybe 10 Nov" into a firm commitment.

## Working with a transcript

Transcripts are long and full of "umm", cross-talk and tangents. Good prompts focus the AI:

```text
Below is a transcript of a 45-minute project review. Extract:
- Key decisions (with who made them)
- Action items: task, owner, deadline (only if stated)
- Risks or concerns raised
- Any disagreements that were not resolved
Keep it under 300 words. Quote the exact words for any decision about budget or dates.
```

Then check: did the summary capture what *you* remember as important? Transcription tools can mishear names, numbers and Indian-English terms, so verify anything critical.

## The follow-up email

The minutes are only useful if people receive them. Ask the AI to turn them into an email:

```text
Turn these minutes into a short follow-up email to all attendees. Start with a thank-you line, list action items with owners and dates in bullets, and ask everyone to reply by tomorrow if anything is incorrect. Friendly, professional, under 150 words.
```

That last request — "reply if anything is incorrect" — is a simple safety net that catches AI mistakes and misunderstandings early.

## Preparing for meetings with AI

AI helps before meetings too:

- **Agenda**: "Create a 30-minute agenda for a meeting to finalise our Diwali sale plan, with time for each item and the decision needed."
- **Briefing**: Paste previous minutes and ask "What is still pending from last time?"
- **Talking points**: "I need to ask my manager for an extra team member. Give me 5 concise talking points and likely objections with responses."
- **Questions**: "What smart questions should I ask in a vendor demo for an inventory software?"

Pooja, a team lead at a Hyderabad IT services company, spends five minutes before every client call asking an assistant to turn the last meeting's minutes into a checklist of pending items. Her calls are shorter and nothing gets forgotten.

## Tracking action items over time

For recurring meetings, keep a running action tracker:

```text
Here are last week's action items and today's meeting notes. Update the action item list: mark items completed, carry forward pending ones, add new ones, and highlight anything overdue.
```

Put the result in a shared sheet or task tool your team already uses.

## When AI summaries go wrong

Watch for these typical errors:

- **Wrong owner** — a task assigned to the person who mentioned it rather than the one who agreed to do it
- **Upgraded certainty** — "we might" becoming "we will"
- **Missed nuance** — a polite objection summarised as agreement
- **Misheard names or numbers** in transcripts

Always skim the summary before sending. You attended the meeting; the AI only processed the words.

## Try it yourself

Allow 15 minutes.

1. Take notes from a real recent meeting, call or even a family planning discussion. If you have none, use the sample notes in this lesson.
2. Paste them into an assistant your company allows (remove anything confidential) with the clean-minutes prompt, including the "do not invent" rules.
3. Check every action item: is the owner right? Is the date actually agreed?
4. Generate the follow-up email and edit it to sound like you.
5. Write a 30-minute agenda for your next meeting using AI.
6. Save your minutes prompt as Prompt #10 in your library.

## Key takeaways

- You can capture meetings with built-in AI, transcripts, or simply your own rough notes.
- Always inform participants and follow company policy before recording or transcribing.
- Tell the AI not to invent owners or dates and to mark tentative items clearly.
- Send a follow-up email that asks people to flag errors.
- Use AI before meetings too — for agendas, briefings and talking points.
