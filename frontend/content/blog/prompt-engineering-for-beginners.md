Prompt engineering is simply the skill of telling an AI assistant clearly what you want, so it gives you useful output the first or second time instead of the fifth. For beginners, it comes down to a handful of habits — giving context, a role, a clear task, a format, examples and constraints — and this guide shows each one with real before/after prompts you can copy and adapt.

## What is prompt engineering, really?

When you type a request into ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot, the AI has only your words to go on. It does not know your job, your audience, your company's style or what "good" looks like to you. A vague prompt gets a generic answer; a specific prompt gets a specific answer.

Prompt engineering is not about magic phrases or secret tricks. It is closer to writing a good brief for a smart new colleague who knows a lot in general but nothing about your situation. The better the brief, the better the work.

## The 6-part prompt framework

You do not need all six parts every time, but for any task that matters, run through this checklist:

| Part | What it means | Example phrase |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Who the AI should act as | "You are an experienced HR manager…" |
| **Context** | Background the AI cannot guess | "…at a 200-person IT services company in Pune." |
| **Task** | The specific job, with a clear verb | "Write a policy announcement about…" |
| **Format** | How the output should look | "Use 3 short paragraphs and a bullet list of key dates." |
| **Examples** | A sample of the style or output you want | "Here is last year's announcement for tone: …" |
| **Constraints** | Limits and things to avoid | "Under 200 words. No jargon. Do not promise new benefits." |

Now let us see it in action.

## Before/after prompt examples

### Example 1: Writing a professional email

**Before:**

> Write an email asking for leave.

The result will be a generic template that sounds nothing like you and may not fit your workplace.

**After:**

> You are helping me write a polite, professional email to my manager at a fintech company. I need 3 days of leave (Wednesday to Friday next week) for my sister's wedding in Jaipur. My current project deliverable is due the following Monday and I will finish my part before I leave. Write a short email (under 120 words) with a clear subject line. Tone: respectful but not overly formal.

Why it works: it gives the **context** (relationship, reason, timing), addresses the manager's likely concern (the deadline), and sets **format** and **tone**.

### Example 2: Summarising a long document

**Before:**

> Summarise this.

**After:**

> Summarise the attached RBI circular for a small NBFC's operations team who have not read it. Give me: (1) a 3-sentence plain-English summary, (2) a table of what changes, who it affects and the effective date, and (3) a list of questions we should ask our compliance officer. If something is unclear in the document, say so rather than guessing.

Why it works: it defines the **audience**, asks for a structured **format**, and includes a **constraint** against guessing — which reduces the risk of confident but wrong answers.

### Example 3: Social media content

**Before:**

> Write an Instagram caption for my bakery.

**After:**

> You are a social media copywriter for a home bakery in Kochi that sells eggless cakes and brownies. Our audience is young families and college students. Write 3 Instagram caption options for a post announcing our new mango cheesecake (available only this month). Each caption: under 50 words, one emoji maximum, end with a call to action to order on WhatsApp. Here is a caption we liked before for tone: "Small batch. Big love. Our brownies are back — DM to pre-order!"

Why it works: **role**, **audience**, an **example** for tone, multiple options to choose from, and tight **constraints**.

### Example 4: Data and Excel help

**Before:**

> How do I calculate sales in Excel?

**After:**

> I have an Excel sheet with columns: A = Date, B = Region (North/South/East/West), C = Product, D = Units, E = Price per unit. I want a formula in column F for revenue, and then a summary showing total revenue by region for each month. Give me the exact formulas, explain each in one line, and suggest whether a pivot table would be easier.

Why it works: the AI knows your exact **data structure**, so it can give formulas you can paste, not a generic tutorial.

### Example 5: Learning a new concept

**Before:**

> Explain blockchain.

**After:**

> Explain blockchain to me as if I am a commerce graduate who understands accounting ledgers but has no technical background. Use one analogy from accounting, keep it under 250 words, and end with 3 quiz questions to check my understanding. Do not give me the answers until I reply.

Why it works: it tailors the explanation to your **existing knowledge** and turns the AI into an interactive tutor.

## Five techniques to level up

Once the framework feels natural, add these techniques.

### 1. Iterate instead of restarting

Treat the first answer as a draft. Follow up with specific feedback: "Make the second paragraph shorter", "The tone is too salesy — make it more neutral", "Add a row for Q4." Conversation is often faster than rewriting the prompt from scratch.

### 2. Ask the AI to ask you questions

For complex tasks, end your prompt with: "Before you start, ask me up to 5 questions that would help you do this better." This surfaces context you forgot to provide.

### 3. Break big tasks into steps

Instead of "Write a complete business plan", go step by step: first the target customer, then the problem, then the offer, then pricing, then marketing. You stay in control and can correct course early.

### 4. Ask for reasoning or alternatives

"Give me three different approaches and the pros and cons of each" usually produces more useful thinking than asking for one answer. For analysis tasks, ask the AI to explain how it arrived at its conclusion so you can check it.

### 5. Save and reuse your best prompts

Keep a personal prompt library — a simple document or note — with prompts that worked well, organised by task. Over time this becomes one of your most valuable work assets. Many assistants also let you save custom instructions or create reusable custom assistants for repeated tasks.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Being too short.** One-line prompts are fine for simple questions, but anything important deserves context.
- **Not specifying the audience.** "Explain this" for a CEO and "explain this" for a new intern should produce very different answers.
- **Trusting facts without checking.** AI can invent figures, references or legal details. Verify anything factual, especially numbers, dates, laws and citations.
- **Sharing confidential data.** Do not paste client data, passwords, or personal information into public AI tools. Follow your organisation's AI policy.
- **Giving up after one bad answer.** A weak answer usually means missing context. Add it and try again.

## A reusable prompt template

Copy this and fill in the brackets:

> You are a [role] helping [who] with [situation/context].
> Your task is to [specific task with a clear verb].
> The audience is [who will read/use this].
> Format: [table / bullets / email / word limit].
> Here is an example of the style I want: [example, optional].
> Constraints: [what to avoid, tone, length, facts to stick to].
> If anything is unclear, ask me before you begin.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is prompt engineering still worth learning as AI models get smarter?

Yes. Newer models handle vague prompts better, but they still cannot read your mind. Knowing how to give clear context, set the format and check the output remains the difference between generic answers and work you can actually use. The skill shifts from tricks to clear communication, which never goes out of date.

### Do the same prompts work on ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Copilot?

Mostly, yes. The core principles — role, context, task, format, examples and constraints — work across all major assistants. You may notice differences in tone or length between tools, so it is worth testing an important prompt on two assistants and comparing.

### Can I write prompts in Hindi or other Indian languages?

Major AI assistants support Hindi and several other Indian languages, though quality can vary by language and task. A practical approach is to try your language first and, for complex or technical tasks, compare the result with an English prompt.

### How long does it take to get good at prompt engineering?

You will see a noticeable improvement within a week of deliberate practice using a framework like the one above. Becoming skilled across different tasks — analysis, writing, coding, automation — typically takes a few weeks of regular use and refining your own prompt library.
