Welcome to AI for Data Analysis, module 1, lesson 3: Data Privacy: Masking Data and What Never to Upload.

Before you paste a single row of company data into an AI tool, you need a privacy routine. This lesson gives you one. It's simple enough to follow every time, and strict enough to keep you, your employer and your customers safe.

First, why does this matter in India specifically? Indian business data is full of sensitive identifiers. There's PAN on invoices, Aadhaar in KYC files, mobile numbers in every CRM, bank account numbers in vendor masters, and salary data in HR sheets. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, places obligations on organisations that process personal data. And most companies also have their own IT policies on which external tools are allowed. You don't need to be a lawyer to act responsibly. You need three habits. Check your company's policy. Share the minimum. And mask what you share.

Rule one: know what your company allows. Some organisations provide an approved AI tool, for example an enterprise version of Copilot or ChatGPT with data-protection terms. Some ban external AI tools altogether. Consumer chat assistants may, depending on the product and your settings, use conversations to improve their models. Enterprise and team plans usually offer stronger commitments. So before using AI at work, find out three things. Which AI tools are approved? Is customer or employee data allowed in them at all? And are there settings you must use, such as turning off chat history or model training? If nobody can tell you, assume the strictest answer, and use only masked or synthetic data.

Rule two: never upload these. Treat this as a hard list. These never go into a consumer AI tool, masked or not, unless your organisation has explicitly approved a tool for that purpose. Aadhaar numbers, including full or partial scans of cards. PAN numbers of individuals. Bank account numbers, IFSC codes with account details, card numbers, and UPI I Ds tied to names. Passwords, OTPs and A P I keys. Salary, medical or performance data linked to named employees. And anything marked confidential, like board papers, unannounced results, or merger and acquisition data. Here's the good news. You almost never need any of these for analysis. A sales analysis needs amounts, dates, categories and cities. It doesn't need to know who the customer is.

Rule three: share the minimum. Before uploading, delete the columns your question doesn't need. If you're asking "which category grew fastest?", you need date, category and amount. You don't need customer name, mobile or address. Work on a copy of the file, and remove those columns entirely. Hiding them isn't enough, because hidden columns still upload.

Rule four: mask what's left. Sometimes you do need a row-level identifier, for example to count unique customers. In that case, replace the real values with codes. Let's look at a few techniques.

Start by replacing names with I Ds. Create a lookup sheet on your own machine that maps each customer to a code, then use the code in the shared copy. In Excel 365, you can list unique customers with equals UNIQUE of the customer name column. Next to that list, you generate codes with a short formula that joins the text "CUST dash" to the row number, formatted as four digits. That gives you CUST 0001, CUST 0002 and so on, assuming the list starts in row 2. The exact formulas are in the lesson notes below the video. Then bring the code into your data with XLOOKUP, and delete the original name column from the copy. Keep the mapping sheet private. It's your key to un-mask the results later. If you're on Excel 2016 without UNIQUE, use Data, then Remove Duplicates, on a copied column instead.

Next, masking mobile numbers. If you must keep a hint of the number, for example to spot duplicates, show only the last four digits. The formula is six X characters joined to RIGHT of the mobile cell, four. So a number like 98765 43210 becomes six X's followed by 3210.

What about PAN, if it's in the file? It's better to delete the PAN column entirely. But if a reviewer needs to see that the format is valid, keep only the structure. A short formula takes the first three characters, adds five X's, and then the last two characters. So a PAN like A B C P E 1 2 3 4 F becomes A B C, five X's, then 4 F. Even so, don't upload it unless there's a strong reason.

And finally, you can shift or band sensitive numbers. For salary or deal-value analysis where exact amounts are sensitive, you can group values into bands, like "five to ten lakh rupees". Or you can multiply every value by a constant factor that only you know. Ratios, rankings and growth percentages stay correct, but the absolute numbers don't leak.

Now here's a powerful pattern: use synthetic data to design, and real data to run. Ask the AI to generate fake data with the same structure as yours. Design your formulas or pivot on that. Then run the finished workbook on the real data, locally. The example prompt in the lesson notes asks for thirty rows of realistic sample data for a Jaipur clothing retailer, with columns like invoice number, date, store, category, quantity, rate, amount including G S T and payment mode. It even asks for two duplicate invoices and three spelling errors, so you can practise cleaning, and it asks for output as a table you can paste into Excel. You'll find the full prompt in the lesson notes below this video. The AI never sees a real customer, and you get a practice file with exactly the messiness you need.

Next, the sixty-second pre-upload checklist. Keep this next to your desk. There are seven questions. One, is this tool approved for this kind of data? Two, am I working on a copy, not the original? Three, have I removed every column the question doesn't need, deleted and not hidden? Four, is there no Aadhaar, PAN, bank, card, password or salary-by-name data? Five, have names, phones and emails been replaced with codes or masked? Six, have I checked the other sheets and tabs in the workbook, and are they clean too? And seven, would I be comfortable if my manager saw exactly what I uploaded? That sixth item catches people often. You clean Sheet 1 and upload the workbook, forgetting the Customer Master tab. The checklist is in the lesson notes so you can copy it.

Now, try it yourself. Spend about fifteen minutes building your masking toolkit. Pause the video and work through these steps. Step one. Ask an AI to generate twenty rows of fake customer data with columns Name, Mobile, City, PAN and Order Amount. Yes, fake PANs in the correct format. This is practice. Step two. Paste it into Excel or Sheets, and create a masked copy. Generate CUST codes, mask mobiles to the last four digits, and delete the PAN column. Step three. Save the mapping from name to code on a separate sheet, and note where you'd store it securely. Step four. Run through the sixty-second checklist against your masked copy. And step five. Find your company's AI or IT usage policy, or ask your manager, and write down which tools are approved.

Let's recap. Check your organisation's policy first. If it's unclear, use only masked or synthetic data. Aadhaar, PAN, bank details, passwords and named salary data never go into consumer AI tools. Delete unneeded columns from a copy, because hidden columns still upload. Replace identifiers with codes, and keep the mapping private. And design with synthetic data, then run your finished workbook on real data locally.
