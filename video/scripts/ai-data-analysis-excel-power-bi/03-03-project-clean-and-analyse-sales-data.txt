Welcome to AI for Data Analysis, module 3, lesson 3: Project: Clean and Analyse a 1,000-Row Sales Dataset.

It's time to put Modules 2 and 3 together. In this project, you'll take a messy one-thousand-row sales dataset from raw export to a clean table and a set of answered business questions. It's the same workflow you'd follow in your first week in an MIS role.

Let's start with the brief. Pink City Fabrics' owner, Mr. Agarwal, wants to understand the last quarter, July to September 2026, before planning his Diwali stock. He's sent you the billing export and five questions. One, what were total sales including G S T, and taxable sales, each month? Two, which categories contribute most to sales, and which grew fastest? Three, how do the three channels compare: the MI Road store, the Vaishali store, and Online? Four, what share of payments comes through UPI, card, cash and cash on delivery, and is cash on delivery rising online? And five, what is the average invoice value, and how many invoices were above ten thousand rupees?

Step one: generate the dataset. You shouldn't practise on real customer data, so you'll generate a realistic file. Use the prompt in the lesson notes with an assistant that can create files. Many can output a C S V or Excel download. If yours can't, ask for a smaller sample, plus the Python or formula approach to expand it. The prompt asks for one thousand rows of sales line items for Pink City Fabrics, from the first of July to the thirtieth of September 2026. It specifies the columns, the three stores, five categories, a simple G S T rule, the amount calculation, payment modes, and a mix of customer cities. Then it adds realistic messiness: about twenty-five duplicate rows, city variants, about forty text dates, a few amounts with an "Rs" prefix, three blank payment modes, and two obvious typo outliers. And it says no real names, phone numbers or I Ds. You'll find the full prompt in the lesson notes below this video. One note: the five percent and twelve percent split by price is a simplified rule for practice. Actual G S T rates depend on the product and current notifications. And if your assistant can't produce the full file, ask it for Python code to generate it, and run that in Google Colab. Lesson 4.4 shows you how.

Step two: profile and clean. Work through the cleaning routine from Lesson 3.1. Create four sheets, named Raw, Clean, CityMap and Cleaning Log. Then work through the checklist in the lesson notes. Record the row count of Raw. Flag duplicates with COUNTIFS on invoice number plus item, review them, remove them, and log the count. Trim, proper-case and map the customer city through CityMap. Convert all dates to real dates, and check that the minimum and maximum fall within July to September 2026. Convert amounts to numbers, removing the "Rs". Flag the blank payment modes, don't guess them, and log them. Investigate outliers. And finally, format the clean data as a Table called Sales.

For the outlier check, add a column that recalculates quantity times rate times one plus the G S T rate, rounds it, and subtracts the recorded amount. Any row where this isn't close to zero is suspect. The formula uses Table syntax, with the at sign, which means "this column, in the same row". It's in the lesson notes below the video. Then decide, with "the owner", which in this case is you, whether to correct or exclude the row, and log it.

Step three: add helper columns. In the Sales table, add four. Month, which is the first day of the month, using EOMONTH. Taxable, which is the amount divided by one plus the G S T rate, rounded to two decimals. G S T, which is the amount minus the taxable value. And Channel Type, which says "Online" if the store is Online, and "Store" otherwise. Format Month as "mmm dash yy", so it displays nicely while staying a real date. All four formulas are in the lesson notes.

Step four: answer the five questions. Use whichever technique fits each one. For monthly sales and taxable sales, build a pivot with Month in Rows, and Sum of Amount and Sum of Taxable in Values. For category share and growth, use a pivot with Category in Rows, Month in Columns, and values shown as Percent of Column Total and Percent Difference From. For the channel comparison, use a pivot or a SUMIFS grid by store and month. For the payment mix and cash on delivery trend, use a pivot with a Store slicer, Payment Mode in Rows, Month in Columns, and Percent of Column Total. And for average invoice value and invoices above ten thousand rupees, you need invoice-level totals. There's a summary table in the lesson notes.

Let's look at that last one, because it's important. Your data is at item-line level, but question five is about invoices. So you need to total each invoice first. There are two options. With a pivot, put Invoice Number in Rows and Sum of Amount in Values. Then copy the result to a new sheet, and use AVERAGE, and COUNTIF for amounts greater than ten thousand. Or in Excel 365, use formulas. UNIQUE lists every invoice number in column A of a new sheet. Then a SUMIFS next to it totals each invoice, referring to the whole spilled list with a hash sign. Then you take the AVERAGE of those totals, and a COUNTIF for those above ten thousand. The formulas are in the lesson notes. Here's the thing. This "grain" issue, line level versus invoice level, trips up many analysts. Whenever someone asks for "average order value", confirm what an order is.

Step five: check with AI, then verify. Once you've got your answers, try a second route. Upload the clean file to an AI assistant with data analysis capability, and ask the same five questions. Lesson 4.1 covers this in depth. Then compare its numbers with yours. If they match, good. If they don't, find out why. Different duplicate handling, date parsing, or line versus invoice confusion are the usual suspects. Resolving the gap is excellent practice, and it builds the verification habit.

Step six: write it up. Create a Findings sheet with three things. First, a small table answering each question. Second, one sentence per question in plain English. For example, "Kurtis grew from X rupees in July to Y rupees in September, the fastest of any category, driven mainly by Online." And third, a Data Notes box, covering rows removed, items excluded, and assumptions made. Keep this file. You'll reuse the clean dataset for the dashboard project and the executive summary in Module 5.

Now, try it yourself. This whole lesson is the exercise, so allow sixty to ninety minutes, in one or two sittings. Pause the video and get started. If you're short on time, do steps one to three now, and steps four to six tomorrow. To call it done, you need three things at minimum. One, a clean Sales table with a Cleaning Log. Two, answers to all five questions, each backed by a pivot or a formula. And three, a one-page Findings sheet with data notes.

Let's recap. Separate Raw, Clean, mapping and log sheets keep your work auditable. Recalculate amounts from quantity, rate and G S T to catch typo outliers. Watch the grain: line-level data needs aggregating before you calculate invoice-level metrics. Cross-check your answers with an AI analysis, and investigate any gap. And always end with plain-English findings and documented assumptions.
