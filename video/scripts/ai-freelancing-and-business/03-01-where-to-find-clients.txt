Welcome to Make Money with AI, module 3, lesson 1: Where to Find Clients: Platforms, Social and Local Networks.

Clients rarely arrive on their own in the beginning. You need a steady, manageable system for finding them. This lesson maps the main channels, which are freelance platforms, LinkedIn, Instagram, WhatsApp and your local network, and helps you choose two to focus on.

Let's start with a simple idea: warm before cold. Think of your client sources in three circles. First, your warm network. These are people who already know you. Friends, family, former colleagues, college seniors, your building society, your parents' business contacts. Second, your lukewarm network. These are people one step away. Friends of friends, alumni groups, community WhatsApp groups, local business associations. And third, the cold market. That's strangers on platforms and social media.

Beginners often jump straight to cold platforms and get discouraged. Your first one or two clients often come from the warm and lukewarm circles, because trust already exists.

Now, your local network. Don't underestimate offline opportunities, especially in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, where fewer people offer these services. Here are four ideas. Walk through your local market. Which shops have an Instagram page that hasn't been updated in months? Talk to businesses you already use. Your tailor, the gym, the pharmacy, your child's tuition teacher. Attend local business meetups, chamber of commerce events, or startup gatherings in your city. And tell everyone you know, clearly and specifically, what you now do. "I set up WhatsApp quick replies and F A Qs for clinics" is easy to remember and pass on.

Next, WhatsApp. In India, a lot of business happens on WhatsApp, so use it respectfully. Use status updates to share your work samples and tips. Contacts who see them may remember you when they need help. In community and business groups, contribute useful answers before ever promoting yourself, and follow each group's rules about promotions. And send direct messages only to people you know, or who've shown interest. Unsolicited bulk messages to strangers are spam. They damage your reputation, and they can get your number restricted.

Now LinkedIn. LinkedIn is effective for B to B services, like content writing, presentations, data work and automation. Search for your ideal clients by job title and location, for example "Founder", "Bengaluru" and "D to C". Engage with their posts thoughtfully for a week or two before reaching out. Post regularly about your niche, so that when you message someone, your profile already shows expertise. And check LinkedIn's job and services features for freelance requests.

Next, Upwork and similar marketplaces. On Upwork, you respond to posted jobs with proposals. Search using specific keywords for your service and niche. Filter for jobs that match your skill level. Many beginners do better with smaller, clearly scoped jobs at first. Read job posts carefully, because clients who write detailed briefs are usually easier to work with. And be selective. Sending fewer, well-matched proposals usually works better than many generic ones.

On Fiverr, buyers find your gigs. Your main jobs are a clear gig, fast responses to enquiries, and excellent delivery, so early reviews are positive. Sharing your gig link on your other profiles can help bring initial traffic.

And Instagram, for local and visual services. Follow and engage with businesses in your niche and city. Comment helpfully on their posts. Share your work using local hashtags and location tags. And when you D M someone, reference something specific about their account.

So how do you choose your two channels? Trying every channel at once spreads you thin. There's a table in the lesson notes to help, and here's the gist. If your niche is local shops, clinics, cafes or salons, start with your local network plus Instagram or WhatsApp. For Indian B to B firms like C As, consultants and startups, use LinkedIn plus your warm network. For international small businesses, try Upwork plus LinkedIn. For packaged, repeatable services, use Fiverr plus one social channel. And for regional language content or subtitles, try YouTube creator communities plus LinkedIn.

Now, a simple weekly client-finding routine. Consistency matters more than intensity. Here's a sample routine for someone with about six hours a week. On Monday, spend an hour finding ten potential clients and adding them to a tracker sheet. On Tuesday, spend an hour engaging with their posts or researching their business. On Wednesday, send five personalised messages or proposals. On Thursday, post one piece of useful content in your niche. On Friday, follow up on earlier messages and update your tracker. And at the weekend, spend an hour improving one portfolio piece or learning one skill.

And track everything. Create a simple Google Sheet with these columns: name, business, city, channel, date contacted, message sent, reply yes or no, next step, follow-up date, and status. Tracking turns outreach from guesswork into a system, and patterns become visible over time. For instance, you might find that clinics reply more than cafes.

AI can help you research, too. Here's the prompt: "I am looking for, type of business, in, your city, that might need, your service. Suggest ten types of signals I can look for on their Instagram, Google Business listing or website that show they could use help."

But don't ask AI to give you lists of specific people's contact details. Collect information yourself from public business sources, and respect privacy.

Now, try it yourself. This takes about twenty minutes, so pause the video and get started.

Step one. Write down fifteen names from your warm and lukewarm network who either could be clients or know potential clients.

Step two. Choose your two main channels, using the table in the notes.

Step three. Create your tracker sheet with the columns we just covered.

Step four. Add at least ten potential clients or contacts to it.

Step five. Block your weekly routine in your calendar for the next four weeks.

Let's recap. Start with warm and lukewarm networks, because trust already exists there. Local, offline outreach works well, especially in smaller cities. Focus on two channels that match your niche, rather than all of them. A short, consistent weekly routine and a tracker sheet beat occasional bursts of effort. And never spam strangers on WhatsApp or scrape personal data.
