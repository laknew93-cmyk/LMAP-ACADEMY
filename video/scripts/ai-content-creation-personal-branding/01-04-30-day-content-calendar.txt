Welcome to AI for Content Creation and Personal Branding, module 1, lesson 4: Plan a Realistic 30-Day Content Calendar.

A content calendar turns good intentions into a plan you can actually follow. In this lesson, you'll use AI to build Project 2. That's a realistic thirty-day content calendar, based on your pillars, your available time, and the Indian festival and work calendar.

Let's start with capacity, not ambition. The most common mistake is planning too much. Someone plans a post every day across four platforms, manages it for ten days, then disappears for three months. Consistency beats volume. A modest plan you keep for a year builds far more trust than an ambitious plan you abandon.

So before you open any AI tool, answer three questions honestly. First, how many hours a week can you spend on content, including editing and replying to comments? Second, which one or two platforms matter most for your audience? And third, which formats are you comfortable with right now? Text, carousel, short video or long-form?

A sensible starting point for most working professionals is three LinkedIn posts a week. Or three Instagram posts plus one reel. A small brand with a part-time social media manager might manage four to five posts a week across two platforms. Choose a number that feels slightly easy.

Next, design a weekly rhythm. A rhythm assigns each pillar or format to a regular day. It removes daily decisions, and it makes your content feel organised to followers.

Let's look at an example. Riya is a UX designer in Pune building her LinkedIn presence. On Tuesdays, she posts a carousel for her expertise pillar, with design tips for product teams. On Thursdays, a story post for her experience pillar, with lessons from projects. And on Saturdays, a short text post for her perspective pillar, with opinions on design careers.

And here's one for a Pune bakery's Instagram. Monday is behind the scenes, with a reel of morning baking. Wednesday is product, a photo post with the story behind the item. Friday is customer love, a repost of a customer story, with permission. And Sunday is weekend specials, with a story series plus one post. Both rhythm tables are in the lesson notes below this video.

Now add dates that matter to your audience. Indian audiences respond to the calendar around them. There are four kinds of dates to consider. First, festivals and occasions. Diwali, Eid, Onam, Pongal, Durga Puja, Raksha Bandhan, and regional new years. Only use the ones that genuinely connect to your audience and brand. Second, financial and work dates. Quarterly results season, G S T and income tax filing deadlines, appraisal cycles, campus placement season, and the start of the academic year. Third, industry events and awareness days relevant to your niche. And fourth, your own milestones. A launch, a workshop, a work anniversary.

Ask AI for ideas, but verify every date yourself. Festival dates shift each year, and tax deadlines can be extended. AI tools may give you outdated or wrong dates.

Now let's use AI to draft the calendar. Paste your voice guide, audience, pillars and idea bank into a chat. Then use the calendar prompt. It asks for a thirty-day calendar starting on a date you choose, for your platforms. It sets constraints. How many posts a week and on which days. Your weekly rhythm. Using ideas from your idea bank first. Relevant dates for your audience in India that month. And a mix of formats. For each post, it asks for the date, day, platform, pillar, format, working title, hook, key point, call to action, and the raw material you need to supply, all as a table. You'll find the full prompt in the lesson notes below this video.

That final column, the raw material you need to supply, is the secret. It reminds you that every post needs something only you can provide. A client story, a photo from your workshop, or a real number.

Next, review the draft like an editor. AI calendars often look tidy but contain problems. Check for five things. First, repetition, where several posts make the same point in different words. Second, weak calls to action, like "Comment below!" on every single post. Vary them. Ask a specific question, invite people to save the post, point to your newsletter, or have no call to action at all. Third, impossible workloads, like three videos in one week when you planned one. Fourth, missing proof. A month with no stories or results feels like lecturing. And fifth, unverified facts or dates.

Then ask AI to fix specific issues. For example, "Posts four and eleven make the same point. Replace post eleven with an idea from the mistakes angle."

Now move it into a working tool. A calendar in a chat window is easy to lose. Copy the table into a spreadsheet, like Google Sheets or Excel, or into Notion, or a simple Trello board. Add a column for status, with stages like idea, drafted, designed, scheduled and published. And add a column for the link once it's live.

Then set up batching. Rather than creating every day, set aside one or two sessions a week. For example, Sunday evening for drafting the week's posts with AI, and Wednesday lunch for designing visuals. Most platforms, and tools like Meta Business Suite for Instagram and Facebook, let you schedule posts in advance.

And leave room for the unexpected. Keep one slot a week flexible, for news in your field or a timely idea.

Now it's time to try it yourself. You'll build Project 2 in about twenty minutes, so go ahead and pause the video. Step one. Decide your weekly capacity, platforms and posting days. Step two. Draw up a weekly rhythm table linking days to pillars and formats. Step three. List three to five dates that matter to your audience in the next thirty days, and verify each one. Step four. Run the calendar prompt with your voice guide, pillars and idea bank. Step five. Review the draft using the editor checklist, and ask AI to fix at least three issues. And step six. Copy the final calendar into a spreadsheet or Notion with status and link columns, and block two batching sessions in your phone calendar.

Let's recap. Plan for the time you really have, because consistency matters more than volume. A weekly rhythm ties pillars and formats to fixed days and removes daily decisions. Use Indian festivals, work and tax dates only where they're relevant, and always verify them. Add a raw material column so every post contains something only you can supply. And review AI calendars for repetition, weak calls to action and unrealistic workloads, then batch your work.
